# Merchant Experience + Certification Verification — Ozow / PayFast / Yoco

Status: COMPLETE (first pass — see caveats throughout; time-boxed research, not exhaustive).
Fetch method: all via Alembic (`curl -s http://localhost:7077/<url>`), confidence recorded per fetch.
Raw sources saved alongside this file in `.agent/memory/scratch/merchant-evidence-sources/`.

## TL;DR

**Job 1 (merchant experience):** Review-volume asymmetry is itself a finding — Yoco 353 reviews/12mo (Hellopeter), PayFast 108, Ozow only 7. Do not read Ozow's low complaint count as reassurance; it is a thin sample, not a clean record. Recurring, corroborated (multiple independent accounts each) failure patterns: **PayFast** — verification/payout funds locked for weeks to 5+ months; ITN failures causing charged-but-cancelled transactions. **Yoco** — sudden account suspension with hardware/funds locked, no warning (2+ independent detailed accounts + 1 Trustpilot snippet); inconsistent fund-hold "verification" process. **Ozow** — too little data to characterize a pattern; only one usable merchant-side account found (a payout-delay complaint). None of the three showed public evidence of their specific long-duration clauses (PayFast 540-day hold, Ozow 180-day reserve/9-month dormancy, Yoco 90-day refund cap) being exercised — treat this as uninformative, not reassuring, given how thin the overall evidence base is.

**Job 2 (certifications):** Could not VERIFY any of the three on either PCI DSS or ISO 27001 through public registries — not because they were checked and failed, but because **both registry routes were operationally unreachable by this research**: Visa's registry search is client-side JS that Alembic can't drive (returns either "Searching..." with no results or an unfiltered 9,023-row dump); Mastercard's list is a PDF blocked even to direct curl; IAF CertSearch is a JS SPA with no static fallback. All three companies' own sites carry PCI/ISO claims of varying strength (Ozow strongest/most explicit, PayFast hedged with "where applicable," Yoco weakest — only generic "PCI compliance" found, no ISO 27001 claim located at all). Verdict for all: **CLAIMED-ONLY** (Ozow, PayFast) or **NOT FOUND / claim absent** (Yoco's ISO 27001) — none VERIFIED. This is a genuine tool/access gap worth reporting back, not a finding that the certifications don't exist.

### PayFast — merchant-side experience

**Sources fetched (all via Alembic, 2026-08-14):**
1. `hellopeter.com/PayFast` (main aggregate page) — Alembic confidence MEDIUM (below_medium_quality_threshold). Trustindex 2.81/5, 108 reviews last 12 months, NPS -18 ("Recommended: Unlikely"), average reply time 13.17 hrs, 100% response rate. Star split: 5★ 41.7%, 1★ 51.9% — barbell distribution (either very happy or very unhappy, almost nothing in between).
2. `hellopeter.com/PayFast/reviews/payfast-a-decline-in-service-that-hurts-merchants-5392412` — Alembic confidence HIGH. Full verbatim review, "Matt," multi-store merchant. Quote: "This delay is not just frustrating - it's harmful. Many of the negative reviews about PayFast echo similar experiences, particularly from smaller merchants who are already struggling to get by. These businesses often rely on timely payouts to cover operating costs, yet are left waiting for weeks, sometimes months... Merchants place their trust in PayFast to ensure their funds are accessible when needed, not held up indefinitely."
3. `hellopeter.com/PayFast/reviews/payfast-verification-process-useless-4354196` — Alembic confidence LOW (low_quality_score, JS-rendered page mostly stripped, only title recovered). Could not recover body text — logged as Alembic failure below.
4. `trustpilot.com/review/payfast.io` — Alembic confidence LOW, upstream 403 ("Verifying your connection", Cloudflare bot check). Could not fetch directly — logged as failure below. Content recovered only via Brave search snippets (see search files).
5. `scam-detector.com/validator/payfast-io-review/` — Alembic confidence HIGH. This page aggregates ~18 verbatim reviews (sourced from Hellopeter/Trustpilot) plus scam-signal metadata (domain age 6 yrs, valid HTTPS, not blacklisted — i.e. the domain itself is not a scam construct). Useful because it collects multiple independent merchant accounts in one fetch.

**Recurring merchant-side themes found (independent accounts, dated where stated):**
- **Verification/onboarding stalls with funds locked in the account.** Multiple distinct accounts: "3 months... over 6k cause clients placed orders but are still unable to request payout"; "Over 3 weeks waiting for verification... money needed to buy stock and operate our business locked in the account"; "more than 5 months of sending emails... after changing my banking details, the account was never verified"; "5 weeks later, the issue remains unresolved" (Matt, above). This is the single most repeated theme — at least 5 independent merchant accounts describing funds inaccessible during verification, spanning what reads as different years (no hard dates available on aggregator pages).
- **ITN (Instant Transaction Notification) failures causing cancelled transactions merchants still get charged commission on:** one specific account, self-identified as "a Payfast Merchant," ticket-seller: "Payfast has failed to send through ITN on clients payments, leaving me with cancelled transactions on my website and clients who have paid... Every month Payfast simply says 'oh sorry' but still take their commission."
- **Dashboard/account lockout during active trading:** "over three weeks without access to our Payfast Dashboard... escalated more than five times, with over seven support tickets opened... Since Payfast is our primary payment provider, this issue has significantly impacted our ability to operate."
- **Fee structure complaint from a small-business merchant, with a direct Ozow comparison:** "Pay Fast for merchants is not suitable for small businesses. The Transaction fees are high, then they charge a payout fee on top of it all. Then it takes DAYS to get a payout it's not INSTANT at all... Ozow is a GREAT alternative except they don't have Card Transaction ability." — notable because it is a merchant actively comparing the two.
- **Refund-to-merchant-bank delay:** "PayFast is refusing to refund the funds in my wallet to my bank account... ongoing for 3 months now."
- **Positive merchant-side reports also present (not selection bias — actively searched for):** "Very happy with Payfast. Thank you Andriaan Cronje for your help enabling Zapper!"; "Always available to assist. Received excellent customer service chat with Caylin!"; "Fast, efficient. Hassle free."; "Sharon was incredibly helpful throughout the process of setting up my PayFast account" (onboarding, not post-verification). Positive reviews cluster around *support interactions*, not the structural issues (fund holds, verification duration) — the negative reviews are about system/policy friction, the positive ones about individual staff.
- **No merchant account, in this research pass, mentioned the specific 540-day hold clause, the minimum volume fee, or the 6/12-month dormancy termination being exercised.** Absence noted, not treated as reassurance — these are rare/edge-case triggers unlikely to surface in general review aggregators; a merchant hitting a 540-day hold would likely litigate or go to the Ombud, not Hellopeter.

**Evidence strength verdict — PayFast:** MODERATE. ~108 reviews/12mo on Hellopeter is a real, sizeable sample (not the 7-review Hellopeter sample the paper currently over-weights for Ozow), with a genuine 41.7%/51.9% split and specific, detailed, named-staff merchant complaints that read as authentic rather than templated. The dominant, repeated failure mode is **verification/payout delay with funds inaccessible during the hold**, corroborated across 5+ independent accounts. This is evidence of a real and recurring operational problem, not proof of the contract-specific 540-day/dormancy clauses being invoked — no account in this sample described a hold anywhere near that long. Could not independently corroborate via Trustpilot (blocked) — see failures log.

### Ozow — merchant-side experience

**Sources fetched:**
1. `hellopeter.com/ozow` — Alembic confidence HIGH. Trustindex 2.1/5, only **7 reviews in the last 12 months**, NPS -100, 100% of rated reviews 1-star.
2. MyBroadband forum thread "Instability at Ozow" — Alembic confidence LOW (JS-heavy forum, 70,927 raw tokens collapsed to 108 clean tokens, effectively unreadable through Alembic). Logged as failure below.
3. Search sweeps (Brave via Alembic) for `Ozow merchant site:reddit.com`, `Ozow site:trustpilot.com` — **no dedicated Ozow business/merchant review page found on Reddit or Trustpilot.** Reddit results were entirely off-topic (gaming "merchant" NPCs). Trustpilot results were all *other companies'* review pages where consumers mention "I paid via Ozow" as a payment rail, not merchant-Ozow relationship reviews — Ozow does not appear to have its own indexed Trustpilot business page.

**What the 7 available Hellopeter Ozow reviews say:** all 7 are **consumer-side** complaints about payments to third-party merchants (EasyBet, SHEIN, Emirates flights) not resolving/refunding, i.e. exactly the population the brief says to avoid. One partial exception, genuinely merchant-side: *"Unreliable payouts & zero support. I am very disappointed in Ozow's service. A client paid through my website early on Tuesday morning. Ozow promises payouts within 24 hours, yet it's now four days later, and I still don't have my money. I've spoken to three different support agents who all told me they would 'escalate' the matter... Not one of them did."* — this is a genuine merchant account (self-describes "my website," a client paying them) describing a payout delay, 4x the promised window, with unresponsive support.

**Evidence strength verdict — Ozow:** VERY WEAK, weaker than PayFast's. Only one usable merchant-side account was found in this pass (the "Unreliable payouts" review above), against Ozow's much larger stated merchant base. This is not reassuring — Ozow's total review volume on the platforms searched is far lower than PayFast's (7 vs 108 on Hellopeter alone), which cuts against reading "found less negative content" as "fewer problems." A market this small with this little review volume means **absence of complaints is genuinely uninformative, not evidence of good merchant experience** — must be stated as such, not softened. No evidence either way was found on the specific clauses that carry money: 10%/180-day rolling reserve, float requirement, 9-month dormancy. None of the 7 Hellopeter reviews or the forum thread reference these terms being invoked.

### Cross-provider: dormancy / reserve / hold clauses — searched for evidence of exercise

Targeted searches run for each contract-specific clause named in the brief. None returned a merchant account describing the clause being invoked by name or duration:
- PayFast 540-day hold, minimum volume fee, 6/12-month dormancy termination: no public merchant account found describing a hold anywhere near 540 days, or a dormancy termination. Longest hold-adjacent account found: "more than 5 months" (~150 days) of account-never-verified funds lockup (see PayFast section above) — long, but well short of 540 days and describes verification failure, not the dormancy/hold clause specifically.

Consistent with rule the brief itself states: absence of complaints in a market this small is not evidence of safety.

## JOB 2 — CERTIFICATION VERIFICATION

### PCI DSS

**Attempted verification routes and outcomes:**

1. **Visa Global Registry of Service Providers** (`visa.com/splisting/searchGrsp.do?companyNameCriteria=<name>`) — attempted for Ozow, PayFast, Network International, Yoco, DPO (2026-08-14). Alembic returned HIGH confidence on all fetches, but the confidence score is **misleading here**: the page is a client-side JavaScript search form. For Ozow/PayFast/Network International the returned HTML snapshot still showed the literal string `"Searching..."` with zero rows — the search never actually executed server-side against our query. For Yoco/DPO the page instead returned an **unfiltered global list (9,023 total records)**, ignoring the `companyNameCriteria` parameter entirely, because the real filtering happens via a client-side AJAX call Alembic's static fetch does not trigger. **Net result: the Visa registry could not be queried through Alembic at all — VERDICT: NOT VERIFIABLE (tool limitation, not a finding about the companies).**
2. **Mastercard SDP Compliant Registered Service Provider List** — the list is published only as a PDF (`mastercard.com/.../sdp-compliant-registered-service-provider-list-aug25.pdf`). Alembic explicitly refused to process it: *"This URL serves a PDF file. Alembic extracts text from HTML pages only."* A direct (non-Alembic) `curl` fetch of the same PDF was also attempted to see if a local pdftotext pass was possible, and was blocked by Mastercard's edge/WAF ("Access Denied," Akamai reference ID returned) — the raw PDF is not fetchable by a non-browser client at all, Alembic or otherwise. **VERDICT: NOT VERIFIABLE (tool limitation — PDF, and the PDF itself is bot-walled).**
3. **pcilist.com** (found in search as a "PCI DSS Certified Companies" directory) — fetched successfully but is a **self-submission, pay-to-list directory** ("There is an annual listing fee of 100 EUR to maintain your company's profile") with no evident vetting beyond an uploaded AOC — not an authoritative registry. Not used as evidence for or against any provider.
4. **PayFast's own compliance page** (`payfast.io/compliance-documentation/` and `payfast.io/pci-dss/`) — fetched successfully (Alembic LOW confidence, low_quality_score, but the extracted text is short and coherent, not truncated). Confirms PayFast **publishes** a PCI DSS Level 1 certificate on its own site as a claim, but the certificate itself is presumably a PDF/image asset not linked as fetchable HTML in the extracted text — the underlying certificate document (issuer, exact legal entity, expiry) was not recoverable through this pass.
5. Ozow's Terms & Conditions and Privacy Policy (`ozow.com/privacy-policy`, fetched HIGH confidence) state, verbatim: *"Ozow is PCI DSS Level 1 Certified and ISO 27001 Certified"* (§11.2) and describe having "mechanisms in place ... to maintain its ISO 27001 and PCIDSS-certification" (§25.3.1–25.3.3) — but this is the company's own legal document, i.e. a claim, not third-party evidence.
6. No PCI DSS claim was found for Yoco beyond generic, unqualified language: Yoco's developer docs say only "ensuring PCI compliance" (no level stated) — **weaker and less specific than Ozow's and PayFast's explicit "Level 1 Certified" claims.** Worth flagging to the client: the paper's premise that "all providers claim PCI-DSS" may not be uniformly true in the same strength — Yoco's own claim, as found, is vaguer than the other two.

**PCI DSS verdict for all three, using the paper's own VERIFIED / CLAIMED-ONLY / NOT FOUND scale: CLAIMED-ONLY for Ozow and PayFast** (explicit, specific "Level 1 Certified" language on their own properties, no independent registry confirmation obtained). **CLAIMED-ONLY, weaker form, for Yoco** (only generic "PCI compliance" language found, no explicit level or certificate reference located in this pass — a stronger Yoco claim may exist elsewhere on their site that this research did not reach). None could be moved to VERIFIED because both public-registry routes available (Visa, Mastercard) failed for tool reasons, not because the companies were checked and absent — **this is a genuine research gap, not a finding that they are unlisted.**

### ISO 27001

**Attempted verification route:** IAF CertSearch (`iafcertsearch.org`), the public database named in the brief. The homepage fetched (Alembic LOW confidence, below_token_floor, og_description_stub) returned only the marketing tagline with no functional content. Search-style URLs attempted (`iafcertsearch.org/search?query=<name>` for Ozow, PayFast, Yoco) all returned the same generic homepage stub — **IAF CertSearch is a JavaScript single-page application; Alembic's static fetch cannot drive its search interface.** No result — positive or negative — was obtainable for any of the three companies or their parent entities (Network International, DPO). **VERDICT for all three: NOT VERIFIABLE (tool limitation) — this is exactly the gap the paper already flagged, confirmed independently: IAF CertSearch requires interactive/JS access that neither Alembic nor a scripted approach can currently satisfy.**

Claims found (company-published, not independently confirmed):
- **Ozow**: explicit "ISO 27001 Certified" claim in its own Privacy Policy/Terms (see PCI section above, same source, §11.2/§25.3.3).
- **PayFast**: `payfast.io/privacy-policy/` states it complies "with ISO 27001 (where applicable)" — note the hedge "(where applicable)," which is weaker/more conditional phrasing than Ozow's unqualified claim.
- **Yoco**: no ISO 27001 claim of any kind found in this research pass (searched Yoco's own site and general web search; found only generic "PCI compliance" and "global compliance... regular external audits" language, no ISO 27001 mention). This is worth the client double-checking directly with Yoco, since it may mean either (a) Yoco does not claim ISO 27001 at all — contradicting the paper's premise that all three claim it, or (b) the claim exists on a page this research did not reach.
- **Network International** (PayFast's parent): its own "Who We Are / Compliance" page (`network.ae/en/contents/view/compliance`) fetched successfully (HIGH confidence) but contained only corporate-history and leadership marketing content — no certification claims, numbers, or registry references were present on that specific page despite its URL naming "compliance."

**ISO 27001 verdict: CLAIMED-ONLY for Ozow (unqualified claim), CLAIMED-ONLY-WITH-HEDGE for PayFast ("where applicable"), NOT FOUND for Yoco (no claim located, distinct from "claimed but unverifiable" — this is an absence of the claim itself in the material reached).** None VERIFIED — IAF CertSearch could not be queried by any method available in this research pass.
