What this factor means

Things the scoring cannot capture

A contract says what they may do, not what they will do. Everything scored here is a legal right a provider has reserved for itself — not a description of how they normally behave. Payment companies write broad discretionary powers into their terms because they carry the risk when a merchant disappears mid-dispute; most of those powers are used rarely, against merchants in trouble, and would look nothing like this in practice for a twenty-year-old orchid council selling show tickets. Read the harsh clauses as worst-case exposure, not as a forecast.

The reverse trap is just as easy. PayFast's terms let it hold funds "until we are satisfied that all sums due have been fully paid" — open-ended — and then cap that hold at 540 days, taking the earliest of three clocks including the card schemes' own dispute window. Eighteen months sounds alarming, and this page initially scored it as the worst term found anywhere. It is the opposite: it is the ceiling on an otherwise unbounded hold, and it tracks card-scheme rules rather than PayFast's whim. Peach and Stitch reserve the same kind of hold and state no limit at all. A stated maximum is a protection; silence is not.

Only Peach puts the price in the contract. Its rates sit in Annexure A of the signed agreement, changeable only by mutual agreement. Ozow, Yoco and Paystack all defer to their public pricing pages — visible, but alterable. PayFast defers to a private "Application" you cannot see until you apply, and its clause 3.4 commits you to a Minimum Volume Fee whenever monthly volume is R20,000 or under, with the amount disclosed nowhere. For a show held once every three years, that threshold is met only during the months when tickets are actually selling, and unmet through the long quiet stretch either side of it.

Yoco's own two contracts disagree with each other. The 2020 Merchant Agreement requires binding arbitration in Cape Town; the 2026 Main T&Cs says no arbitration and any court. The liability cap differs too. Ask Yoco which document governs before signing anything.

Yoco has no 3-D Secure statement for online checkout. All seven are confirmed PASA-registered from the regulator's own public register — that part is independently verified, not marketing. But Yoco is the only one with no discoverable 3DS commitment for card-not-present payments; its security material is all about card machines. SAOC's ticket sales are 100% online, and 3DS is what shifts fraud liability away from you. Ask before signing.

It compounds: Mastercard publishes a register of every service provider that has filed a PCI Attestation of Compliance, with the auditor's name and date. Ozow, PayFast, Peach and Paystack are all on it. Yoco is not. That is a checkable absence, not proof of non-compliance — Yoco may rely on its acquiring bank's certification, or simply not submit to Mastercard's list. But it is the one registry that could actually be queried, and Yoco is missing from it while its four competitors are present. Ask Yoco directly for its PCI status and its AOC.

Two caveats on the others. Paystack's attestation is filed under Paystack, Inc., the global entity — not confirmed as covering Paystack South Africa, which is who you would contract with; and its own compliance page cites PCI DSS 3.2, an older standard than Peach's stated 4.0.1. Every provider's level and version remains its own word: Mastercard's list records that an attestation exists, not what it says. Ask each for the document.

Nobody has an account anywhere. The PayFast integration on the website is a developer sandbox, not an SAOC account. All ten start from zero, all ten need FICA, and the deadline is the end of August — about two weeks. Only Flutterwave publishes an approval turnaround, at roughly 72 hours; the others publish nothing at all. So "can we actually be live in time" is the one question no amount of further research will settle — it needs an email to each of them.

The last three are thinly documented, and their greys are honest. iKhokha, Zapper and PayGenius came out of a census of thirty-five providers, and all three clear the same bar as the rest: self-serve sign-up, a published rate, no monthly fee on the base tier. But far less of their paperwork is public. iKhokha's only obtainable e-commerce agreement is a 2021 template whose fee schedule is printed blank; PayGenius's General Terms could not be retrieved at all. Those grey cells mean "not established", not "bad" — but a provider whose terms you cannot read before signing is itself a fact worth weighing, and each is at least as likely to improve as to worsen once they answer.

Flutterwave was nearly left out entirely. It was never on the original list. An audit of who had been excluded, and why, found it had a South African registration, a competitive published rate and a non-profit sign-up option — the same signals that justified a proper read of Peach and Paystack, both of which had also been dropped without anyone opening their contracts. It is here because leaving it out would have repeated that mistake. Read its scores carefully though: it builds fastest of the ten, and its contract holds ten percent of your takings for six months.

One unanswered question could move PayFast a long way. It is the only provider with a non-profit product — a Cause account with "reduced fees" — but the rate is published nowhere, and the marketing is written around donations while SAOC sells tickets. If that discount reaches ticket sales, PayFast goes from dearest to cheapest. Worth an email before you decide.

What to ask each provider, in writing

  • All except Flutterwave: how long does approval take for a registered NPO, from submission to live? None publish it.
  • PayFast: what is the Minimum Volume Fee amount, does the Cause rate apply to ticket sales rather than donations, and what is that rate? Also: clause 21.2(iii) refers to a Termination Fee in clause 21.3(ii), which does not exist in the document — what is the fee?
  • Yoco: which contract governs, the 2020 Merchant Agreement or the 2026 Main T&Cs? They conflict on arbitration and on the liability cap.
  • Ozow: clause 20.8.1 has SAOC consent, on its ticket buyers' behalf, to Ozow marketing to them and to their data being processed across borders, on an opt-out basis. Can it be waived? SAOC cannot give that consent for its members.
  • Peach: what is the KYC turnaround, is there a bespoke NPO rate, and is there a cap on how long funds can be held during a dispute? The contract sets none.
  • Paystack: the contract allows termination on 24 hours' notice and disclaims any uptime guarantee. What happens if that falls during the final days of ticket sales, or across the show itself?
