1Summary and Recommendation

This research is ours — we built it for SAOC. But nothing here is locked in. The Council decides. We looked at Ozow first and hardest because someone flagged it as a real option. That depth does not mean we recommend it. All the numbers are from 14 August 2026. Check with the vendors yourself before you sign anything.

You need a payment gateway by the end of 2026. Your 2027 Show is three days and you need to let people buy tickets in advance. We looked at six companies. We cared about three things: cheap, refunds that actually work, and being trustworthy with money.

Recommendation — rewritten 14 August 2026, after reading both contracts

The answer depends on one decision the Council has not yet made: are you going to sell society memberships through the website?

If yes — stay with PayFast. It is the only one that can bill memberships automatically month after month. And those monthly payments solve a second problem at the same time, explained below.

If no, and this stays a ticket-only account — PayFast is a poor fit and Ozow is the better contract. Ozow is cheaper, and you can leave on 30 days' notice. PayFast ties you in for three years at a time and charges you a monthly fee in every month you sell little or nothing — which, for a Show held every three years, is nearly every month.

The thing that decides it: does the account get used between Shows?

Both companies write their contracts for businesses that trade every month. SAOC does not. That mismatch is where the real money and the real risk sit.

PayFast charges a "Minimum Volume Fee" in any month where you take R20,000 or less. Clause 3.4 The Show runs every three years. In roughly 35 months out of every 36, SAOC would take nothing at all — and pay that fee every one of those months. The amount is set in your application form, not in the contract, so we cannot tell you what it would be. You must ask.

It gets worse before it gets better. If the account is unused for 6 months, PayFast may suspend it and keep charging fees. Clause 21.2(i) At 12 months, the agreement ends by itself — no letter, no warning — and they may charge a termination fee. Clause 21.2(iii) That clause points at "clause 21.3(ii)" for the amount. There is no clause 21.3(ii) in the document. The fee is real, the amount is nowhere. Ask them what it is, in writing.

Now the good news, and it is genuinely good. If SAOC sells memberships by monthly subscription, the account is busy every single month. The Minimum Volume Fee stops applying once monthly income passes R20,000. The dormancy clocks never start. The three-year lock-in stops mattering, because you would be using the account for three years anyway. The membership plan does not just add revenue — it fixes the contract problem.

That is why the recommendation splits the way it does. It is the same contract; whether it suits SAOC depends entirely on whether the account is used between Shows.

Three reasons pull toward PayFast:

  1. The code is already written and tested. PayFast's security layer has been reviewed. Switching gateways three weeks before you open ticket sales means throwing away that work and rebuilding it in a rush. That is real risk for no clear gain.
  2. PayFast handles both tickets and memberships. If you sell society memberships through the website (which you are thinking about but have not decided yet), PayFast is the only one that handles one-off tickets and recurring charges on the same account.
  3. PayFast knows non-profits. They have it built into their signup. We already have an account. The code is live and ready.

Must resolve before go-live

The strongest argument against PayFast is real and must be raised with PayFast directly: PayFast's Clause 9.8 permits them to hold sale proceeds for up to 540 days following delivery, and Clause 9.6 lists twelve discretionary triggers for doing so. For a body selling tickets weeks before an event, this is a genuine risk. The Council cannot proceed without a written commitment from PayFast on this point.

Some smaller downsides: PayFast does not push refund updates automatically. The system has to ask "Is the refund done yet?" instead of being told. We can do this with the same security code that already guards the payment flow, so it is not much extra work. PayFast's developer docs are solid but their example code is in PHP, not our language. That is easy to work around.

Ozow is cheaper — roughly R8 per R500 ticket, no matter how people pay. Their refund system actually works. They are adding safer ways to connect to banks (no more login passwords shared with a payment company). What we do not yet know is whether they will take on a national council like SAOC — that is a question for them, not something their published material answers.

What Ozow's contract holds that its website does not show

Ozow's merchant agreement runs to 34,265 words V1.2026, effective 1 April 2026. Two clauses in it change the picture materially, and one is a straight mismatch with how SAOC operates. Neither appears anywhere in Ozow's public pricing or product pages — you would only find them by reading the contract.

Ozow and PayFast, side by side

We have now read both companies' full merchant agreements — the actual contracts you would sign, not their marketing pages. Ozow's runs to about 34,000 words and PayFast's to about 23,000. Everything below comes from those two documents. Where we quote a clause number, it is so you or a lawyer can look it up; you do not need to read the clause to follow the point.

Plain summary before the detail: neither company is a villain, and neither is a soft touch. They protect themselves in very similar ways. Ozow is cheaper. PayFast fits how SAOC actually works slightly better in some places and much worse in others. The differences that matter are about money you cannot get at and what happens in the years between Shows.

What does it cost us?

Ozow

About R8 on a R500 ticket. The cheapest of everything we looked at, on every payment method. This is Ozow's big advantage and it is real.

PayFast

Noticeably more, especially on card payments. Both quote prices before VAT, so add 15% to whatever you are quoted.

If a member cancels, can we refund them?

Ozow

Yes — but only if there is money sitting in your account with them to pay it from. If there isn't, the refund doesn't happen until you put money in. Ozow decides how much you must keep parked there, and can ask for more at any time. Clauses 7.2, 7.3

PayFast

Same catch, different wording. PayFast can refuse to process a refund until you have deposited the money into your bank account for them to draw. It is at their sole discretion. Clause 11.10

Can they hold on to our money?

Ozow

Yes. They can keep back up to 10% of your monthly card takings for 180 days as a safety buffer, and can extend that if they get nervous. It does not end when you leave. Annexure 3, clauses 9.5–9.7, 16.2

PayFast

Yes, and for longer — up to 540 days, though it ends sooner if the window for disputes closes first. Twelve listed situations let them do it. They can also ask you for a cash deposit or bank guarantee as security. Clauses 9.6, 9.8, 20.1

What happens in the three years between Shows?

Ozow

If the account sits unused for nine months, they may suspend or close it. Not automatic — they have to decide to do it and tell you. Clause 4.5.1

PayFast

Worse. Six months unused and they may suspend you and still charge fees. Twelve months unused and the agreement ends automatically — no warning letter required — and they may charge you a termination fee for the privilege. Clauses 21.2(i), 21.2(iii)

Are we tied in?

Ozow

No. Either side can walk away on 30 days' written notice, for any reason. Clause 4.3.1

PayFast

Yes — three years. The agreement runs for 36 months and then renews itself for another 36 unless you write to them at least 3 months before it rolls over. Miss that window and you are in for another three years. Clause 21.1

When do we actually get the money?

Ozow

The contract does not say. It promises only "reasonable endeavours" and gives no number of days for any payment method except crypto. It also says they are not liable if settlement is late. Get a timeline written into your order form. Clauses 7.1.1, 8.2.1

PayFast

Paid out on a frequency set in your application form — so it is at least written down somewhere. But they reserve the right to change that frequency by giving you notice. Clause 9.1

If someone disputes a payment

Ozow

R350 plus VAT each time, and that fee can change. On a R150 ticket, one dispute wipes out more than two ticket sales. Annexure 3, clause 9.3

PayFast

The disputed amount comes straight back off you, whether or not they ask first, and they can take it from your bank account. If disputes get frequent they can suspend you. Clauses 11.4, 11.9

Do they want a non-profit like us?

Ozow

We are not on their risky-business list — that covers lending, crypto, forex and gambling, not orchid societies. Good news. But they can still label any customer "high risk" based on their own judgement, and the money-holding rules above apply either way. Schedule 2, clauses 2.1, 2.2

PayFast

Their website says they sign up non-profits, and SAOC already has an account — so in practice, yes. But the contract itself says nothing about non-profits at all, exactly like Ozow's. Whether they take you is their call either way. No clause on this

Could we sell society memberships through them too?

Ozow

Only through Capitec Pay, so only for members who bank with Capitec. Not a real option for the whole membership.

PayFast

Yes. Still the only one that handles both one-off ticket sales and recurring membership subscriptions on a single account.

Does the contract tell you what you will be charged?

Ozow

Partly. Some fees are fixed in the contract — the R350 dispute fee, for instance. Others point at prices published on their website, which they can change without asking you.

PayFast

No — not one fee amount appears in it. The transaction fee, the monthly minimum fee, the payout fee and the termination fee are all set in a separate "Application" form we have not seen. You cannot work out what PayFast costs from the contract you sign. Ask for the Application form before signing anything.

What if the payment system goes down mid-Show?

Ozow

There is a service-level schedule setting out what they aim for. Worth reading, but check what you actually get if they miss it. Schedule 4

PayFast

Nothing is promised. There is no uptime commitment anywhere in the contract, and they specifically say the service will not necessarily be uninterrupted or error-free. If it fails on your Show weekend, you have no claim beyond a very small cap. Clause 19.3

If they cause you a loss, what can you recover?

Ozow

Capped at roughly twelve months of the fees you have paid them. Meanwhile what you owe them is uncapped — that imbalance is normal in this industry, but it is worth seeing.

PayFast

Capped at about two months of fees — six times tighter than Ozow's. On a Show that only sells for a few weeks, two months of fees is a small number.

The thing that worries us most — what happens between Shows

Both companies can close an account that sits unused, and the Show only runs every three years. So the account will sit unused for a long time, every time.

PayFast is the stricter of the two. Six months of no sales and they may suspend you while still charging fees. Twelve months and the agreement ends by itself — nobody has to send you a letter — and they may charge a termination fee on the way out. Ozow gives you nine months, and someone there has to actually decide to close you and tell you.

Either way, SAOC would likely need to set the account up again before each Show. That is worth knowing now rather than discovering three weeks before tickets go on sale. Ask both companies, in writing, whether they will make an exception for an organisation that runs a big event every three years. They may well say yes. But get it in writing.

Ozow started in 2014 and is licensed by the financial regulator (PASA) to move money. We looked at it first and hardest because you flagged it as a real option. This depth is because we wanted to be thorough, not because we think it is better.

Bank-access mechanism — good news and work still happening

Ozow connects to your bank to take refunds and set up recurring charges. We should be honest about how that works, because it matters.

Here is what is real:

  1. Capitec does it the safe way. You never give Ozow your password. This is how online banking should work.
  2. FNB and RMB have just switched too (late July 2026). No passwords needed. This is recent and real.
  3. For Standard Bank, Absa and Nedbank, the older way still applies. Your member logs into their bank online and Ozow does a payment for them while they are there. The regulators warned against this in 2020, because it asks members to share their passwords.
  4. Ozow says it is safe. They report no fraud since 2014. (This is Ozow's own number, not third-party verified.)
  5. But they are fixing it anyway. Ozow is switching banks over one by one to the safer way.

Recommend asking Ozow

Ask Ozow directly which banks are credential-free today, and whether there is a timeline for completing the migration. The company's public commitments suggest movement; the monthly change rate determines whether the risk is acceptable before sales open.

International cards — you have to ask for it

Ozow's main product works for South African bank accounts. International visitors cannot use it.

Ozow has a separate card product for Visa and Mastercard (3.5% + R1). You have to tick a box when you sign up to turn it on. If you do not, visitors from outside South Africa will see "payment failed". This is not on by default.

Refunds — they work, but there is a catch

Ozow has a real refund system: you can issue full or partial refunds through a dashboard or API, and the money goes back to the account that paid. This is good for privacy — you never collect your members' bank details separately.

The catch — must resolve before go-live

Ozow pays refunds out of a pot of money they hold for you. If that pot is empty, refunds stop until you put more money in. This is not a guideline; it is how they work.

Refunds are processed twice a day (09h00 and 12h00), Monday to Friday only. Your member sees the money in three days.

Cost: R3 per refund, plus you do not get back the original Ozow fee. A R500 EFT ticket cost you R7.50 to process. Refunding it costs you roughly R10.50 in total (R7.50 lost + R3 refund fee) plus you return the R500. (This figure comes from a reseller's published copy of Ozow's terms — check it with Ozow yourself.)

Before you sign up: understand how to put money in the pot and what happens if it runs out when you are processing lots of refunds. Get this in writing from Ozow.

Recurring billing — Capitec members only

Ozow has one way to take recurring charges: Capitec Pay. Your member agrees once, then Ozow automatically takes your membership fee at the times you set, up to a limit they choose.

But Capitec Pay only works for Capitec account holders. If your member banks with FNB, Absa, Standard Bank or Nedbank, there is no recurring option.

Your member can cancel at any time in their Capitec app. Ozow tells you afterwards, not before. Your system needs to handle mid-cycle cancellations when they happen.

When you actually get the money — three different answers

Ozow's website says:

  • "Next-day settlement" (pricing page)
  • "Same-day for EFT" (FAQ)
  • "Once a week for cards" (FAQ)

These do not match. Get Ozow to write down, in the contract, exactly how many days for each payment method. Do this before you sign anything.

Signing up as a council — not yet clear

Ozow's signup guide mentions only two paths: "Company" (registered business + business bank account) and "Informal" (personal ID + address proof).

SAOC is neither. The company estimates signup takes 3 hours to 2 business days. Before you commit, ask Ozow directly: what documents do you need from us and which path do we use? This is not a showstopper, just an unknown.

Compliance claims — check them

  • Ozow says they meet PCI-DSS Level 1 (payment data security). But they have not published their certificate number or an expiry date.
  • Ozow says they meet ISO 27001 (data security). No supporting paperwork.

(Section 9 shows you how to verify these before you sign.)

What other companies say about them

Hellopeter (a review site) shows Ozow at 2.1 out of 5 stars. Seven reviews, all one-star. People say they have to wait for payouts and refunds.

But take this with salt: seven reviews is tiny. These are from customers, not merchants. People only review when they are angry. Do not overweight it. But do not ignore it either. Before you commit, search online for what other merchants say about Ozow — especially about how they handle busy seasons and refund backlogs.

What the actual contract says — the good and the rough

We got the real contract: Ozow Merchant Terms and Conditions V1.2026, dated 1 April 2026. 34,000 words. Every fact below is quoted from that document. When we cite a clause, you can look it up.

They can hold back your card takings Annexure 3, clauses 9.5–9.10. Ozow can hold up to 10% of your monthly card sales for 180 days as a safety buffer for disputed payments and penalties. Two problems: first, this applies not just to "high-risk" merchants but to anyone Ozow decides is risky. Second, Ozow can make the holdback bigger or longer if they see dodgy transactions or too many refunds — with no limit written into the contract. If you end the relationship, the held money does not come back faster.

For a Show this is real money trouble: you take all the ticket money in three days, then six months later you get it back, after the venue bills are paid and the event is over.

They can freeze you for nine months of inactivity Part 2, clause 4.5.1. If nothing happens for nine months, Ozow can shut down the account. This is a real problem for a council that only runs a Show every three years (covered fully in section 1).

They can take their fees from your money without asking first clause 3.5. Ozow can deduct what you owe them from any money they hold for you, and they do not have to warn you first.

Liability is one-way clauses 9 and 10. If Ozow messes up, you can only recover up to twelve months of processing fees. If you messes up (or Ozow says you did), your obligation to Ozow is unlimited. Ozow can take over your legal defence themselves and charge you the full cost — the expensive end of the cost scale.

They can shut you down for subjective reasons clause 5.1.1. Ozow can suspend you immediately if they think you are doing something "prejudicial" to them, or "threatening to" their relationships, or damaging their reputation. This could happen in the middle of your Show.

By signing up, you agree they can email your members about special offers clause 20.8.1. You are giving Ozow permission to market to your ticket buyers and members. That is permission you cannot actually give on their behalf.

You are the responsible party for member privacy clause 20.5. You are legally responsible for how member data is handled. (This is true regardless of which gateway you choose. Ozow does not collect bank details for refunds, which is good — but it does not mean you have fewer privacy obligations overall.)

Hidden costs — things not on the price list

Ozow's public pricing is clear and honest. But the contract has extra costs that the price list does not show. Plan for them.

  • Pass-through fees clause 6.7 — if a bank charges Ozow, they charge you. You only hear about it on the invoice after the fact. This list is open-ended.
  • Penalty fee on top of the penalty Annexure 3, clause 9.4 — if a card scheme fines Ozow, Ozow adds 1.05% of the fine as a handling charge and passes it to you.
  • Chargeback fee — R350 (no VAT) per dispute clause 9.3 — this can change.
  • Refund fees Annexure 1, clause 6.4 — these exist but are not written in the contract. Ozow points to their website instead, which means they can change them without telling you.

The big pattern: most of the fees are not fixed in the contract; they are linked to Ozow's website, which means they can change whenever Ozow wants without a new agreement.

Things Ozow can change whenever they want

Reading the full contract, most of the big decisions sit with Ozow and can change without much notice. Fee increases get 14 days notice; big contract changes get 30 days. But Ozow can change the money they hold back, the size and length of that holdback, whether you are "high risk", your transaction limits, and their reasons to shut you down — all at their discretion, often based on what Ozow thinks is "reasonable".

This is normal for payment companies — they hold the fraud risk. But it is why you must nail down answers to key questions in the signed contract, not in an email from a salesman. Email does not count. Only the signed contract counts.

What to do before you sign

None of the above is unusual. Payment companies protect themselves this way everywhere. But two things should be fixed in writing before you commit: the nine-month freeze (does Ozow make exceptions for councils with three-year cycles?), and whether they would hold back 10% of your card takings (do they hold back for non-profits selling event tickets?). Both can be negotiated. Do not assume the standard terms.

Also: have a lawyer read clauses 9, 10 and 23 (arbitration) before you sign. This is normal practice when you have uncapped liability, not a reflection on Ozow.

3The Main Alternatives

PayFast (the one you have now)

PayFast is owned by a big London payment company. The technical code for PayFast is already written and running live. It is testing with fake money right now.

Pricing (confirmed ex-VAT):

PayFast pricing
MethodFee
Cards3.2% + R2.00
Instant EFT2.0%
Capitec Pay2.0%
Apple Pay / Google Pay / Samsung Pay3.2% + R2.00
SnapScan3.5% + R2.00
Zapper4.5% + R5.00
Mobicred3.2%

No setup fee, no monthly fee. PayFast is consistently more expensive than Ozow across every payment method modelled, by R3.50–R8.50 per transaction depending on size and method.

When you get the money: 48 hours after the payment clears, then 1–2 more working days to your bank. R8.70 per payout (no VAT). If you ask for money every day you pay R8.70 daily. If you ask once a week you pay R8.70 weekly — batch your requests to save money.

Refunds — API documented. PayFast has a fully documented Refunds API reachable at https://api.payfast.co.za/refunds/:

  • GET /refunds/query/:pf_payment_id — query what is refundable and by which method
  • POST /refunds/:pf_payment_id — create a refund (full or partial)
  • GET /refunds/:pf_payment_id — retrieve refund status

Authentication uses the same MD5-hash-plus-passphrase scheme SAOC's existing ITN webhook already implements and has had security review. There is no refund webhook, so refund status must be polled. There is no official Node/TypeScript SDK, only a PHP one; the REST API is straightforward and can be wrapped in a few lines.

Refund costs and timing: R2.00 per refund (no VAT), flat. But you do not get back the original fee you paid to process the payment. So refunding a R150 card ticket costs you R6.80 (the lost original fee) plus R2.00 (the refund fee) = R8.80 total. Cheaper tickets hurt more proportionally. Buyers wait 7–10 business days for card refunds, 2–3 days for bank-transfer refunds.

Bank details problem

If you refund EFT payments, you need bank details from your member. Card refunds go back down the card rail automatically. EFT refunds do not. You have to collect and keep their account holder name, bank name, branch code, account number and account type. This means you have to store and protect members' bank details — that is a privacy law obligation on you, not PayFast (see section 9). Some transactions cannot be refunded at all.

Explicitly onboards non-profits — account type selectable at signup. FICA requirement: proof of ID, address and bank account.

PayFast owns SnapScan, Zapper and Mobicred and embeds them inside its payment menu at no extra integration cost — a settings toggle, not a new vendor relationship. Card refunds are available for all three.

Recurring charges for memberships: PayFast has this built in. You can set up subscriptions (regular charges every month or year) and card-on-file (store a card and charge it when you want). One downside: if the card expires, PayFast sends the member a link to update it — it does not happen automatically. Renewals are not fully hands-off.

Security when PayFast talks to your system: PayFast uses an older hashing method (MD5) which is weaker than what newer companies use. But they also check where the message came from (your IP address), which is a real strength. Your existing code already does this and passed security review.

Yoco

Yoco actively signs up non-profits — you can use your chairperson, treasurer or secretary to apply. They have a whole path for NPCs. The fit is actually good for SAOC.

They allow event ticketing. It is not on their banned-activities list.

Cost: 2.95% flat, no monthly fee. (Other tiers at R249 or R499 per month for lower rates, if you do higher volume.)

Real cost on a R150 ticket: 2.95% = R4.43 vs R6.80 on PayFast. Cheaper.

The dealbreaker

Yoco cannot do recurring charges for memberships. That kills it if you want to sell memberships. Yoco admits this in their own docs. So Yoco is only for tickets, not for memberships.

Refunds — does not work for your pattern. Yoco can process refunds (it has an API and everything). But here is the problem: Yoco settles your money every single day. Refunds come out of the money they still hold. If someone asks for a refund four weeks after the Show, and you have not had any other sales in the meantime, the Yoco pot is empty and the refund fails. Yoco's own help docs say "wait for new sales" or "send them the money yourself from the bank and tell us to forget the refund". For a council that takes all the ticket money in three days then has zero sales for weeks, this is broken. Also: Yoco only refunds things for 90 days, and debit-card refunds only work on the same day you received the payment. Yoco is honest about these limits in their docs, but they disqualify Yoco for your use case.

Webhooks use HMAC-SHA256 with replay protection and versioned signatures — more modern and stronger than PayFast's MD5.

PCI compliance is asserted but no level is stated and no certificate is provided — weaker disclosure than PayFast.

No official Node/TypeScript server SDK exists.

Peach Payments

Peach says no monthly fee on their Growth plan. (You might see R300/month mentioned online — that is for enterprises doing R500k+ per month.)

Cost:

  • Cards: 2.95% + R1.50
  • Bank transfer / Capitec / Nedbank: 1.50% + R1.50
  • You get paid the next business day

Refunds: built in properly. Peach actually tells your system when a refund is done (that is the strongest refund support of any company we looked at). Real engineering went into this.

Security: stronger hashing than PayFast (SHA256 instead of MD5), plus they check your IP address and retry failed messages automatically.

Unknowns: no example code in your language. Whether they sign up non-profits is not documented. They market to big companies, not orchid councils.

Paystack (owned by Stripe, but separate)

Paystack is in South Africa and owned by Stripe, but it is its own product. You do not get Stripe's support team or reliability promises. Do not assume Stripe will bail you out.

Cost (in rands, add 15% for VAT):

  • Local cards: 2.9% + R1
  • International cards: 3.1% + R1
  • Capitec and other bank transfers: 2% flat
  • Small fee waived on payments under R10
  • R3 per payout
  • No monthly or setup fee
  • You get paid in 2 business days

Refunds: best documented of everyone. Full or partial refunds work, you can issue multiple partials for one payment, and Paystack tells your system when it is done. If a refund needs the member's bank details, Paystack tells you. Members wait up to 10 business days for refund money.

Unknowns: whether they accept non-profits is not documented. Whether event ticketing is allowed is not documented. You have to ask.

4Every Option We Looked At — and Why It Is In or Out

PayFast — in, but only if memberships go ahead

What it is: The payment system SAOC already uses. A buyer pays by card or bank transfer, and the money lands in SAOC's bank account.

Why it's in: One reason that survives scrutiny — it is the only option that can bill memberships automatically, month after month. Nothing else on this list can. If SAOC sells memberships online, that settles it.

5Refunds — A Cross-Cutting Technical Reality

Why cheap payments are hard to refund

This is how payment systems work: cheap payment methods are hard to refund.

Cards (Visa, Mastercard) have a built-in reversal system. Refunds are easy. Bank transfers are cheaper because there is no middleman. But with no middleman, there is no system to push money backwards. So refunds are hard.

Money must be there to refund it

Both PayFast and Ozow have the same rule: if there is no money, no refunds. This is not a weakness of either one; it is how payment systems work.

  • PayFast: takes the refund from money they are holding for you. If that is not enough, they can take it from your bank account — but they can also refuse the refund until you put the money in first.
  • Ozow: keeps a pot of money for you for refunds. If the pot is empty, no refunds until you top it up.

Real talk: neither is better than the other. Both need money in the system. Pick a gateway and keep some money set aside for refunds, whether it is sitting in Ozow's pot or in your own bank account.

Quirks of each payment method

  • Capitec Pay: once a payment is done, it cannot be reversed. To refund, you send money back as a new payment.
  • PayShap: you can only issue one refund per transaction. A partial refund uses up your one shot. If someone asks for a second refund on the same ticket, you have a problem.
  • Apple Pay and Google Pay: these refund via card rails, same as credit cards. Your staff may need the virtual-card number ending, not the physical card number.
  • Zapper: refunds work via API. SnapScan standalone (outside PayFast) has no refund path confirmed. Avoid it if refunds matter.

Privacy: who needs your member's bank details

Ozow doesn't need them. Ozow sends refunds back to the same bank account that paid. SAOC never touches members' bank details.

But PayFast EFT refunds do

Card refunds work like Ozow — the money goes back down the card rail. But EFT refunds are different. You have to collect and keep your member's banking details: account holder name, bank, branch code, account number, account type. This means you are storing personal banking data, which is a privacy law obligation. If SAOC uses PayFast and enables EFT refunds, this data-handling must be secure and properly tracked (see section 9).

Your system cannot track refunds yet

The word "refund" does not exist in your ticket code. Your ticket states are: reserved, paid, cancelled, checked-in. No refund state.

Right now, when someone asks for a refund:

  1. You issue it in the PayFast or Ozow dashboard.
  2. You manually change the ticket to "cancelled" in Firestore.
  3. You write it down somewhere (or forget).

The system does not know if a ticket was cancelled before payment or after a refund. They look the same.

When does this become a problem? Each refund takes 10–20 minutes of volunteer time. 20 refunds = 3–7 hours. 200 refunds (maybe 10% of tickets) = 30–65 hours during the busiest week of the year.

You do not need this before sales open. But you need it before sales close. Build it before summer.

This is your system's problem, not the gateway's. Either way, you need to fix refund tracking to make this work.

6Tickets and memberships need different things

You do two different things:

  1. Show tickets: one-off sales, many in a short burst, lots of refunds right before the event, then nothing for three years.
  2. Memberships: small regular charges spread across the year, members come and go, need to cancel easily to keep members happy.

These pull you toward different gateways. Tickets want cheap (EFT, bank transfers). Memberships want easy recurring billing.

No single gateway is great at both.

PayFast: handles both. Has documentation for recurring charges.

Yoco: tickets only. No recurring billing.

Ozow: recurring only for Capitec (one bank). Everyone else, you have to build it yourself.

Peach and Paystack: unknown for recurring.

This research assumes you want both. That means PayFast, or Ozow with extra work.

Platforms hold your money until after the Show

Every South African ticketing platform (Quicket, uTickets, Webtickets, Howler) does the same thing: they hold all your ticket money for 2–3 days after the Show ends. Quicket sends it out 2–3 days post-event.

Real problem for you

You usually pay a venue deposit weeks before the Show. Ticket money has to get to you fast enough to cover it. Platform money arrives after the Show is over.

One exception: uTickets pays you the next business day after the Show, and lets you withdraw early if you ask. But uTickets is new and small (R2 million processed, about 500 events total). More risk than Quicket.

Platforms do not do memberships

Not a single South African ticketing platform (Quicket, uTickets, Webtickets, Howler, Computicket/TicketPro) offers recurring membership billing.

So platforms do not replace a gateway. They add to one. You would need:

  • Quicket for tickets
  • A separate gateway for memberships
  • Two separate vendor contracts
  • Two different fee schedules
  • Member data in two places

This mess favours the custom build, which uses one gateway for both tickets and memberships.

Custom vs. platform: the real trade-off

Custom code is cheaper in rands per ticket. Platforms are cheaper in your volunteer time. Which matters more is your call, not ours.

But here is what actually moves the needle: 84% of South African online shoppers abandon their cart. If your checkout is missing their preferred payment method, they leave. The fee difference (2–3%) is nothing compared to a broken checkout.

Stop asking "which is cheaper?" Start asking "will our members actually finish the payment?"

8VAT — is the price the full price or not?

Some companies quote prices ex-VAT (before the 15% tax), others incl-VAT (after the tax). The pricing pages do not always say which. This matters.

Clearly labelled as ex-VAT

  • PayFast: says "ex VAT" right on the page. Add 15% for the real cost.
  • Yoco: says "ex VAT" in their help centre. 2.95% + 15% VAT = 3.39% real cost.

Not labelled — we are guessing

This matters for fairness

  • Ozow: no label on the price page. Probably ex-VAT based on one other mention in their contract. But we cannot be sure. If Ozow is actually VAT-inclusive, they are cheaper than we show in the tables. So we might be making Ozow look worse than they are.
  • Peach: no label. Unknown.

What this means for you

  • If SAOC is VAT-registered: you can claim back the VAT you paid on gateways. The ex-VAT numbers are what matters. A R1,500 PayFast card payment costs R50 in fees (ex-VAT).
  • If SAOC is not registered: you pay the full 15% VAT and cannot claim it back. That same R1,500 ticket costs R57.50 in fees (incl. VAT).

To do: ask your treasurer if SAOC is VAT-registered. We will also ask Ozow and Peach to confirm ex-VAT or incl-VAT in section 11.

9Security, Compliance and the Law

Payment card security (PCI-DSS)

All six companies say they meet PCI-DSS. None publish their certificate number or expiry date. This is normal. Before you sign, ask them for the certificate and check it on the IAF public register.

PCI-DSS certifies how a company operates, not just a product. A vendor can be certified for one part of their business and not another.

Your setup now is the safest: your site sends customers to PayFast's site to enter their card. Card data never touches your servers. PayFast handles the card data. You handle login security, session management, and webhook verification.

New rules came in 31 March 2025. Two apply if you ever change:

  • Scripts on payment pages must be secure
  • You must detect tampering

But these only apply if you put payment fields on your site. If you keep sending to PayFast's site, you are fine.

Do not move card fields to your site

If anyone suggests putting card fields on your own website for convenience, say no. It creates new security obligations. Only do this if the Council explicitly approves.

POPIA — privacy law and who is responsible

You are the responsible party for member data. The gateway is the operator — they help you handle it.

The law says you must sign a contract with the gateway requiring them to keep member data secure. Get a POPIA operator agreement signed with whichever gateway you choose.

If there is a data breach, you have to tell people. The gateway should tell you immediately when they spot it, so you can meet your legal deadline.

PayFast EFT refunds mean you store bank details

If you use PayFast and refund EFT payments, you have to keep members' bank account details (name, bank, branch code, account number, type). This means you have a POPIA storage obligation. Your security procedures must be written down and auditable. Other gateways (Ozow, Yoco, Peach, Paystack) handle refunds without needing member bank details. If you pick PayFast for EFT refunds, this data-handling must be properly managed.

ISO 27001 — data security management

All six companies claim ISO 27001. None publish the certificate number, registrar, or what exactly is certified.

ISO 27001 is a certificate that a company has proper information-security processes. A vendor can be certified for one part of their business and not another. Ask for their certificate and check it on the IAF register.

SOC 2 — do not expect this

SOC 2 is a US security audit, not a certificate. South African gateways almost never have it. Do not ask for it.

What the gateway's security does not cover

No matter how secure the gateway is:

  • You are responsible for HTTPS on saoc.co.za (encrypted traffic)
  • You are responsible for admin login security
  • You are responsible for verifying that messages from the gateway are real
  • You are responsible for how member names, emails and phone numbers are stored in your system

The gateway's certification covers their side, not yours.

Can customers cancel and get refunds by law?

South African law gives some online buyers a cooling-off period. Here is where your tickets stand:

Consumer Protection Act, section 16: a 5-day right to cancel applies to unsolicited offers. Your members are asking to buy tickets — that is not unsolicited. This does not apply.

Electronic Communications and Transactions Act, section 44: a 7-day cooling-off period applies to online purchases.

But section 42(2)(j) says no refund right on entertainment at a specific date. A ticket for the Show on a fixed date falls in that exclusion.

Bottom line: South African law does not give customers a right to cancel Show tickets. Your refund policy is your own (subject to the Consumer Protection Act, section 17).

Section 17 says you can charge a cancellation fee if it is reasonable. Reasonable means: you gave notice, you cannot resell the seat, you had real costs. One exception: zero cancellation fees apply if the person has died or been hospitalised.

Get a lawyer to review your refund policy

This is our reading of the law based on the actual text. It is not legal advice. Before you publish a refund policy, have an attorney review it.

Chargeback — no protection with bank transfers

Bank transfers (Instant EFT) cannot be charged back. There is no dispute mechanism at the bank level.

Good for you (no chargeback fees, no fraud). Bad for your member if you refuse a refund — they have no bank-level recourse. Only your good faith protects them. This is a reason to publish a fair, generous refund policy and actually follow it.

What your refund policy should say

Publish a refund policy that:

  1. Explains that South African law does not give a cooling-off period for Show tickets.
  2. Lists your cancellation fees by date (the closer to the Show, the higher the fee). Justify the fee with real costs: venue deposit, catering commitments, staff time.
  3. Waives cancellation fees if the buyer has died or been hospitalised.
  4. Commits to full refund (no fee) if SAOC cancels or postpones the Show.
  5. States how long each refund takes (card refunds 7–10 days, bank transfers 2–3 days, etc.).

10aMissing pieces of the custom ticket system

Before the Council can sell tickets, the custom system needs these five things. None of this depends on which gateway you choose:

  1. Real money testing: PayFast is set to test mode (fake money). Switch to real transactions, run through the whole flow (buy ticket → payment processes → system creates ticket), and verify it works end-to-end.
