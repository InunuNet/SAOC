# Research → seeded copy map

Every seeded block on this page traces to a numbered finding in
`.agent/memory/project/show-exhibitor-conventions.md`. If a block cannot be traced, it does not
ship. This table is the answer to "why does the page say this?" — and the reason nobody has to
take the copy on trust.

**Reading the Status column:** see `exhibitor-confirmation-model.golden.md`. `research` means
established international practice offered for correction; `pending` means we are holding a slot
open for a value only SAOC can give; `question` means the research looked and found nothing.

---

## The copy rules that bind every block below

1. **No sentence may be readable as a statement of SAOC policy.** The grammar carries this, not
   just the badge. Use *"Most orchid shows require…"*, *"International practice is…"*,
   *"SAOC's committee has not yet set…"* — never a bare *"You must…"* or *"Entries close…"*.
   The contract asserts no seeded section body contains an unhedged imperative of the form
   "You must" / "Exhibitors must" / "Entries close on".
2. **No invented number.** No date, no time, no fee, no ownership duration, no dimension, no
   percentage presented as SAOC's. Where the research supplied a number belonging to a named
   foreign show, it is quoted *with the attribution attached* — "the Orchid Society of Great
   Britain requires twelve months" is a fact about OSGB and is safe; "plants must be owned for
   twelve months" is an invented SAOC rule and is not.
3. **No wild-orchid content.** SAOC is orchids in cultivation. Habitat, fieldwork and
   indigenous-species conservation link to `wildorchids.co.za` and are not described here.
4. **No duplication** of the show classes (already on `/national-show`) or the judging standards
   (already on `/judging`). Those blocks link and summarise in one sentence; they do not restate.

## Section blocks

### `entryProcess` — status `research`
> **Heading:** How entry works
>
> Orchid shows around the world run entry the same broad way: you register your plants ahead of
> the show under an exhibitor number, and each plant is entered against a numbered class from a
> published show schedule. Registration usually opens well before the show and closes before
> staging begins; some shows also accept on-the-day entries, and some do not.
>
> One point is near-universal and worth knowing in advance: **matching your plant to the right
> class is the exhibitor's job, not the show committee's.** Judges may reclassify or disqualify a
> plant entered in the wrong class. Established shows publish a genus-to-class cross-reference to
> help with this.
>
> SAOC's own entry process for the 19th National Show has not been published yet.

Source: research §1 (Santa Barbara, OSWP, AOS *Guide to a Successful Orchid Show*).
Note the last line: the block ends by naming its own gap rather than trailing off as if complete.

### `fees` — status `pending`
> **Heading:** Entry fees
>
> Entry fees for the 19th National Show have not been set. Shows structure them differently —
> some charge per plant entered, some a flat fee per exhibitor, and shows affiliated to an
> accredited judging body sometimes add a separate fee for formal award judging on top of the
> show entry itself. Which of these SAOC will use is for the show committee to decide.

Source: research §1. The AOS three-line-item model (show fee / trophy fee / award fee) is
explicitly *"one model to offer, not the default"* per the research — so it is described as one
option among several, never as the structure.

### `classes` — status `research`
> **Heading:** Classes
>
> Show classes are the categories your plants compete in. They are normally organised by genus or
> alliance — Cattleya and its relatives, Oncidiinae, Paphiopedilum, Phalaenopsis, Vandeae and so
> on — with separate classes for single plants and for groups, plus catch-all classes for genera
> that do not fit elsewhere. Many shows also run a novice or first-time-exhibitor class, and
> judges may split a class that attracts a large number of entries.
>
> **The classes for this show are listed on the show overview page.**

Source: research §3 (OSGB Section A, OSWP cross-reference, OSCOV).
**Hard rule:** this block links to `/national-show` and does not list a single class code. The
`showClass` documents are the single source. Asserted: no class code pattern in the seeded body.

### `judging` — status `research`
> **Heading:** Judging
>
> At most shows, judging takes place after staging closes and before the public are let in, with
> exhibitors not present while the judges deliberate. Larger shows commonly run two tracks at
> once: the show's own placings — first, second, third — and separate formal awards assessed
> against a points standard by accredited judges.
>
> Two conventions worth knowing: an exhibitor who does not want a plant formally judged can
> normally say so, and individual plants inside a society display are usually **not** entered
> into individual judging unless the exhibitor marks and registers them separately.
>
> **SAOC's judging standards are published in full on the judging pages.** Whether exhibitors may
> be present during judging at the National Show has not been confirmed.

Source: research §4 (Sunset Rotary "Do Not Judge", AOS judging FAQ, OSGB display-marking rule).
**Hard rule:** links to `/judging`, restates no criterion, no points scale, no award tier.

### `eligibility` — status `research`
> **Heading:** Plant condition and eligibility
>
> Two requirements are effectively universal across judged orchid shows:
>
> **Free of pests and disease.** Entries are inspected, and a plant that fails inspection is
> removed from the bench. This protects every other plant in the hall, so it is enforced strictly
> everywhere.
>
> **Correctly labelled.** Your label needs the full name — genus and species, or the full hybrid
> name with its parentage. Where a hybrid has never been formally registered, the accepted
> practice is to give the trade name, or the genus name followed by the word "hybrid", rather
> than leaving it blank. A plant that cannot be identified generally cannot be judged.
>
> Many shows also require that you have **grown the plant yourself for a minimum period** before
> it is eligible — the Orchid Society of Great Britain, for example, requires twelve months'
> possession for its main classes. The principle is widespread; the period varies from show to
> show, and SAOC's has not been set.

Source: research §5. The twelve-month figure appears *only* as an attributed OSGB fact, per the
research's explicit instruction to *"leave the number for SAOC's committee."*

### `display` — status `research`
> **Heading:** Displays and stands
>
> Society and group displays are usually judged as their own category, separate from individual
> plant classes, with each group given a defined amount of floor space. Where shows allow
> non-orchid plants in a display for staging effect, they normally cap them — the Orchid Society
> of Great Britain limits ornamental material to half the exhibit.
>
> Practical questions we could not answer from published show rules anywhere: who is allowed to
> build a display stand, whether height or structural limits apply, and what materials are
> permitted. These are listed in the questions below.

Source: research §6, plus §6's explicit *"flag as a real gap, not a convention to assume."*

### `sales` — status `research`
> **Heading:** Selling plants
>
> Every show we looked at keeps retail plant sales structurally separate from the competitive
> floor: vendors work from their own trade area with their own setup process, and that is a
> different arrangement from entering plants for judging.
>
> Whether an exhibitor may sell plants directly from a competitive entry is a different question,
> and no published show rules we found address it either way. It is one for the committee.

Source: research §7. The deleted "Orchids & More" South African page is *not* cited — the
research flagged it unverifiable, so it earns no weight here.

### `practicalities` — status `question`
> **Heading:** Insurance, security, watering and loading
>
> This is the part of exhibiting that published show rules are quietest about. Overnight security
> for benched plants, who waters them during the run, insurance and liability, and where
> exhibitors load and unload were **not covered in any public exhibitor document we could find** —
> not because shows have no arrangements, but because those arrangements tend to live in internal
> committee paperwork rather than on a website.
>
> We are not going to guess at SAOC's. Each of these is listed as an open question below, and the
> answers will be published here once the show committee has settled them.

Source: research §8, quoted almost directly — *"a genuine gap in the public record, not a 'no
convention exists' finding."*

### `permits` — status `question`  ⚠ the block most likely to do harm if got wrong
> **Heading:** Permits and moving plants between provinces
>
> **CITES does not apply to moving your cultivated plants around South Africa.** CITES governs
> international trade — import, export and re-export across a national border. Bringing plants
> from one province to another for a national show is not international trade, and no CITES
> paperwork follows from it. CITES would only come into play if a plant were being brought into
> South Africa from abroad for the show.
>
> What we cannot tell you is whether any **domestic** rule applies — a provincial plant-movement
> or agricultural-biosecurity requirement unrelated to CITES, or any documentation specific to
> propagated indigenous species such as Disa or Eulophia. We searched and found nothing, but
> finding nothing is not the same as confirming there is nothing. Both are open questions for the
> committee below.
>
> Growing and showing orchids in cultivation is SAOC's remit. For southern Africa's wild orchids,
> see Wild Orchids of Southern Africa at wildorchids.co.za.

*(Wording note for @dev: `check-policy-language.mjs` rejects the words "habitat", "in the wild",
"wild-collected", "conservation status" and "red list" anywhere in the seeded copy — that
vocabulary is WOSA's remit, not SAOC's. Link out; do not describe. The sentence above is phrased
to satisfy that check; keep it that way.)*

Source: research §11 (CapeNature CITES permits page, SAFLII CITES Regulations 2010, CITES World
newsletter #9 exemptions for artificially propagated hybrids).

**Why this block is a `question` and not `research` even though the CITES finding is verified:**
the *negative* — CITES does not apply domestically — is verified and stated plainly, because
leaving it vague would let a rumour fill the gap and frighten exhibitors out of entering. The
*positive* — what, if anything, does apply — is unknown, and that is what drives the block's
status. The contract asserts this block carries the question marker and that the word "CITES"
never appears within the same block as an unhedged requirement.
