Uniting conservation, science, and horticulture to explore how orchids in their natural environments inform responsible growing, breeding, and the sustainability of the orchid industry.

The National Show Symposium will feature an outstanding programme of presentations by respected local and international speakers covering orchid cultivation, breeding, conservation, taxonomy, judging, sustainability and emerging scientific research.

Presenters

Speaker

Robert Fuchs

Bob Fuchs is a third-generation orchid grower, born and raised in Homestead, Florida. He has been involved in growing orchids since childhood. Bob was 10 years old when he first experienced collecting orchids in the wild. He accompanied his grandfather, Fred Fuchs Sr., to explore some of the many swamp and wilderness areas that were then abundant in South Florida.

Read full bio of Robert Fuchs

Grandpa Fuchs would tie a rope around Bob’s waist, attach it to his own belt, and off they would go. Bob remembers falling many times, with his grandfather using the rope to pull him out. The rope served a double purpose: it prevented Bob from getting lost and kept him out of the ‘gator holes.

Bob was 14 when he went on his first orchid salvage-collecting trip outside the country. He, his father, Fred Fuchs Jr., and two friends flew in a small, twin-engine plane from Homestead Airport to the Dominican Republic. The plane was running low on fuel, so they had to land in Santiago rather than continue to the capital, Santo Domingo. They had been unable to contact the Santiago control tower but landed anyway because the fuel situation was critical.

The plane was met by an escort of military jeeps carrying soldiers with machine guns. However, when it stopped on the runway, Bob’s father held out a bucket of Kentucky Fried Chicken to the soldiers. They smiled, put down their guns, escorted the Americans to the authorities, and then allowed them to stay and salvage orchids for three days.

Since then, Bob has made many trips to Central and South America in search of orchids growing in the wild.

During one of his many trips to Nicaragua, Bob spotted an unusual orchid flowering on a large rock outcrop. He climbed through the ravines to get a better look and found a clump of the plant measuring four feet in diameter. Convinced that it was a natural hybrid of Schomburgkia wendlandii and Laelia rubescens, he carefully photographed it, collected a sample, and pressed an inflorescence for later identification.

The pressed inflorescence was submitted to Marie Selby Botanical Gardens and was later identified as Schombolaelia fuchsii, named in honour of its discoverer. Two years later, when Bob returned to the same area to collect additional divisions of the plant, he discovered that the area had burned and that the original plant had been lost forever.

Bob and his father discovered another new species in Bolivia, Catasetum fuchsii.

Bob’s interest in orchids continued to grow, and by the time he graduated from high school, he had amassed quite a collection. His parents gave him a greenhouse as a graduation gift. His involvement with orchid societies also began during this period. He and his three brothers entered their first orchid exhibit at the 1959 Miami Show, sponsored by the South Florida Orchid Society. Their display, entitled “The Fuchs Boys”, won a trophy. Bob joined the society in 1960.

After graduating from Florida State University in 1970, Bob embarked on a teaching career. He taught art at Homestead Junior High School and chaired the school’s Enrichment Department. On weekends and after school, he worked in his grandparents’ greenhouses, trying to clean and renovate them, while also starting his own orchid business on their property.

After 15 years of teaching, Bob retired to devote himself full-time to R.F. Orchids and incorporated the business.

Bob spent many years collecting and studying orchids in various parts of Central and South America. He is currently involved in conserving and perpetuating the native orchid species of Southeast Asia through a breeding programme at his growing facility in Thailand.

An accredited American Orchid Society judge, Bob travels extensively throughout the world to attend orchid shows and present lectures and programmes to orchid societies and other groups. He has also written numerous articles for orchid publications and authored the section on “Vanda Culture” in the American Orchid Society’s Handbook on Orchid Culture.

In 2012, Bob was inducted into the Florida Agriculture Hall of Fame. He is the only orchid grower to have received this prestigious honour.

Bob is a Life Member and former trustee of the American Orchid Society, and served as its President for the 2020–2022 term. He is also a Life Member and past president of the South Florida Orchid Society, as well as a recipient of its Distinguished Service Award.

In recognition of his achievements in orchid hybridising, the Royal Horticultural Society of Thailand awarded him a Life Membership. He is also a Life Member and past president of the East Everglades Orchid Society, and a member of the Royal Horticultural Society.

Speaker

Hildegard Crous

Hildegard Crous’s lifelong passion for South African flora began to take shape in 1990, when she was awarded the Kirstenbosch Scholarship. This marked the beginning of her long-standing association with South Africa’s indigenous plants, particularly the fascinating genus Disa.

Read full bio of Hildegard Crous

In 2000, Hildegard established her own Disa collection and private laboratory, the Cape Institute of Micropropagation, at her home in Barrydale. Through this work, she has dedicated herself to the cultivation and propagation of these remarkable orchids, combining practical horticultural experience with a strong interest in in vitro culture.

Hildegard has shared her knowledge and expertise by presenting practical courses in laboratory techniques and in vitro culturing to various institutions, including the Ministry of Agriculture in Mauritius and the Chikanda Orchid Project at Copperbelt University in Zambia.

Her work with Disa orchids and in vitro culture has also taken her to orchid conferences and international events around the world, where she has presented her research and experience. These include:

* European Orchid Congress, Italy (2006) * National Orchid Show, South Africa (2006) * National Orchid Show, South Africa (2009) * Cape Orchid Society’s 50th Anniversary Conference and Show (2010) * IBSA Symposium, South Africa (2011) * European Orchid Congress, Hungary (2012) * International Orchid Conservation Congress (IOCC5), Réunion (2012) * 21st World Orchid Show, South Africa (2014) * European Orchid Congress, London (2015) * European Orchid Congress, Paris (2018) * International Orchid Conservation Congress (IOCC7), Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew (2019)

Hildegard is also the co-author, together with Graham Duncan, of the book Grow Disas, which shares practical knowledge about cultivating these distinctive South African orchids.

In recognition of her knowledge and contribution, Hildegard was invited in 2023 to join the Board of Trustees of GRBG, an invitation she accepted.

Through her dedication to Disa orchids, her teaching and her international contributions, Hildegard continues to share her passion for South Africa’s unique botanical heritage and the conservation of its indigenous orchids.

Speaker

Tinus Oberholzer

Tinus Oberholzer’s passion for orchids began in high school when he purchased his first plant, which, he is pleased to say, is still alive today. This early interest developed into a lifelong commitment to orchid growing, horticulture and the appreciation of rare plants.

Read full bio of Tinus Oberholzer

After matriculating from an agricultural high school, Tinus studied horticulture at Pretoria Technikon, where he obtained his BTech degree in 2007. He subsequently worked as a senior horticulturist for several years before deciding to establish his own business.

Together with Nollie Cilliers, Tinus founded Plantae Orchids, an orchid and rare plant nursery dedicated to supplying the South African market with the latest hybrids and sought-after orchid species. Through the nursery, they aim to make an exciting range of orchids and unusual plants accessible to local growers and collectors.

Tinus’s involvement with the Orchid Society of Northern Transvaal, now known as the Northern Gauteng Orchid Society (N.G.O.S.), led him to pursue orchid judging. He qualified as a judge with the South African Orchid Council (SAOC) in 2008 and subsequently served on the SAOC Board for three years as President.

His contribution to the orchid community also includes show organisation. Tinus served as Show Chairman of the 15th National SAOC Orchid Show, hosted by the Rustenburg Orchid Society.

In September 2013, Tinus and Nollie published Anyone Can Grow Orchids, a book written to guide and encourage South African orchid growers and gardeners. Sharing knowledge has remained an important part of Tinus’s work. Through presentations to orchid societies, garden clubs and conferences, he enjoys helping others develop their own interest in orchids and discover the rewards of growing them.

Beyond cultivation and judging, Tinus has a particular appreciation for orchids growing in their natural habitats. He and his companions take every opportunity to explore the outdoors in search of wild orchids, combining his professional knowledge with a personal enthusiasm for seeing these remarkable plants in nature.

Through his work at Plantae Orchids, his involvement in orchid judging and show organisation, and his commitment to education and sharing knowledge, Tinus continues to contribute to the South African orchid-growing community.

Speaker

Michael Tibbs

Michael Tibbs has been a member of the American Orchid Society (AOS) since the age of 16, a relationship spanning approximately 48 years.

Read full bio of Michael Tibbs

His involvement with the AOS judging system began while he was working in Tokyo and commuting from London, where he was already an accredited Royal Horticultural Society (RHS) judge. For many years, he was fortunate to visit New York monthly. With encouragement from the late Arnie Linsman, Gary and Linda Kraus, Terri and Roger Williams, and Carlos Fighetti, he joined the AOS judging system.

In 2000, following his relocation to Cape Town, South Africa, Michael transferred to the West Palm Beach Judging Centre and continued travelling monthly from Cape Town to Miami. During this period, he received considerable support and hospitality, for which he has expressed deep gratitude.

Michael is also an accredited orchid judge in South Africa. In this capacity, he tutored and encouraged the Kenya Orchid Society to develop a world-class judging system based on the AOS model, with the aim of elevating the recognition of African orchids. Together, he and his colleagues helped introduce AOS-style judging to Africa over the past three decades.

A regular visitor to Japan and Taiwan, Michael has participated as a judge in most, if not all, AOS judging sessions held in Taiwan over the past 10 years, alongside Paul Bechtel and often George Hatfield. His most recent participation was at National Cheng Kung University (NCKU) in November of the previous year.

Orchids have been the focus of Michael’s professional life. He currently owns and operates The Exotic Plant Company, a nursery in the Netherlands near Rotterdam. He has registered close to 1,000 orchid hybrids with the RHS.

Michael has authored or co-authored approximately 15 books covering various aspects of orchids. These include monographs and checklists produced in association with the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, on Paphiopedilum, Cymbidium and Phragmipedium. He also co-authored The John Day Scrapbooks with Dr Philip Cribb.

His 2007 book, Orchids: A Practical Guide to Care and Cultivation, was adopted by the American Horticultural Society as its go-to reference on orchids and sold 104,000 copies. Subsequent editions and updates have been used extensively by trainee judges, incorporating the latest intergeneric tables.

Michael has maintained a long association with the AOS as an exhibitor, participating for more than 10 years in the Greater New York Orchid Society shows until they were discontinued following the events of 11 September 2001. He has also exhibited at the Philadelphia Flower Shows and the South Florida Orchid Shows, as well as at several AOS Spring and Fall Members’ Meetings. At these events, he has exhibited orchids and delivered lectures on a variety of topics, including his conservation project in Rwanda.

Several of Michael’s articles have been published in Orchids, the AOS magazine, and photographs of orchids receiving AOS awards have featured on its covers over the years.

Michael has also played an active role in the World Orchid Conferences (WOCs), working full-time on the conferences in Japan and Glasgow and serving on committees for the WOCs in Johannesburg and Dresden. At the 2014 WOC, his gold-medal-winning exhibit explored the history of the World Orchid Conferences. He is believed to be one of the few people to have attended every WOC since the 8th conference, held in Frankfurt in 1975.

For the WOC in Dresden, Michael updated his historical exhibit to include memorabilia from the very first WOC. This achievement was made possible through the assistance of many AOS members, particularly the late Tom and Marion Sheehan, as well as contributions from orchid enthusiasts and supporters around the world.

Speaker

Elbert Wijaya

Manager, Breeder, Operations Director

Elbert Wijaya is an orchid grower, breeder and horticultural professional whose work combines a passion for exceptional orchids with the practical demands of large-scale commercial production. Originally from Jakarta, Indonesia, Elbert earned a Bachelor’s degree in Chemistry in Singapore before pursuing his career in orchid cultivation and breeding.

Read full bio of Elbert Wijaya

Since 2017, Elbert has been based in Taiwan, where he serves as Manager, Breeder and Operations Director at Ten Shin Gardens Co., Ltd. In this role, he oversees a commercial greenhouse operation that produces and sells approximately 100,000 plants annually. The collection encompasses around 50 orchid genera, reflecting the breadth of the nursery’s horticultural interests and commercial activities.

Ten Shin Gardens also maintains a strong international presence, participating in approximately 30–35 orchid shows each year across North America, Europe and the Asia-Pacific region. This extensive involvement in international exhibitions provides opportunities to share outstanding plants with orchid enthusiasts, connect with growers and breeders, and explore the diversity of orchids cultivated around the world.

Elbert’s particular horticultural interests lie in Cattleya and Phalaenopsis species, especially plants with exceptional form and colour. He is also passionate about rediscovering rare and difficult-to-find orchids, including unusual species that challenge conventional expectations and broaden our understanding of orchid diversity. Among his interests are blue-flowered Dendrobium species and Papilionanthe tricuspidata, a species once thought to be extinct.

His current and future breeding and cultivation projects focus on section Latouria of the genus Dendrobium, particularly species originating from New Guinea. This group is notable for its striking flowers and horticultural potential, with Papua New Guinea forming a central part of its distribution. Through his work with these orchids, Elbert continues to explore the possibilities offered by rare species and to develop a deeper understanding of their cultivation and breeding.

With his background in chemistry, experience in commercial orchid production and dedication to orchid diversity, Elbert brings a valuable combination of scientific understanding and practical horticultural expertise to the international orchid community. His work at Ten Shin Gardens reflects a commitment to producing outstanding plants while continuing to explore the remarkable diversity and potential of orchids.
