Presenters

Sample sequence. Lee-Ann will confirm the order.

  • Elbert Wijaya

    Manager, Breeder, Operations Director

    Elbert grew up in Jakarta and earned his Bachelor’s degree in Chemistry in Singapore. Since 2017, he has been based in Taiwan, where he oversees a commercial greenhouse that produces and sells approximately 100,000 plants annually. The operation features around 50 orchid genera and participates in 30–35 orchid shows each year across North America, Europe, and the Asia-Pacific region. His primary horticultural passions lie in Cattleya and Phalaenopsis species, with a particular focus on superior form and colour. Elbert is driven by a love for rediscovering rare and hard-to-find orchids—such as blue-flowered Dendrobium species (for example, Dendrobium sp. with blue flowers, you know the species name!), and the once thought-to-be-extinct Papilionanthe tricuspidata. Currently, his ongoing and future projects centre on section Latouria Dendrobiums from New Guinea, a group known for large, striking flowers and a distribution centred in Papua New Guinea.

  • Hildegard Crous

    In 1990 Hildegard Crous won the Kirstenbosch Scholarship. This was the start of her long association with South African flora, including the disas, in 2000, she started her own disa collection and private laboratory, Cape Institute of Micropropagation at her home in Barrydale.

    She has given several tuition courses on practical laboratory techniques and in vitro culturing to various institutions including:

    the Ministry of Agriculture in Mauritius

    Chikanda orchid Project at the Copperbelt University, Zambia,

    Presentations given on Disa and in vitro culture include:

    • The European Orchid Congress 2006 in Italy
    • National Orchid Show in South Africa 2006
    • National Orchid Show in South Africa 2009
    • Cape Orchid Society’s 50th Anniversary conference and show 2010
    • IBSA Symposium 2011 in South Africa
    • The European Orchid Congress 2012 in Hungary
    • International Congress on Orchid Conservation “IOCC5” in Reunion 2012
    • The 21st World Orchid Show in South Africa 2014
    • The European Orchid Congress 2015 in London
    • The European Orchid Congress 2018 in Paris
    • International Congress on Orchid Conservation “IOCC7” at Kew Royal Botanic Garden 2019

    She is co-author of the “Grow Disas” book with Graham Duncan,

    2023 Hildegard was requested to become a member of the GRBG Board of Trustees, - she has accepted.

  • Michael Tibbs

    Michael Tibbs has been a member of the AOS since he was 16 years old, some 48 years.

    All of this started while working in Tokyo and commuting from London where he was already an RHS Judge at the time. He was fortunate to be able visit New York on a monthly basis for many years, and with the encouragement of the late Arnie Linsman, Gary and Linda Kraus, Terri and Roger Williams and Carlos Fighetti, he joined the AOS Judging system.

    Then he transferred to the West Palm Beach Centre in 2000 when he was relocated to Cape Town South Africa and continued commuting from Cape Town to Miami on a monthly basis. I helped his AOS journey by hosting him for weeks and sometimes months that he expressed a lot of gratitude for.

    He is also an accredited judge in South Africa during which he tutored and encouraged the Kenya Orchid Society to build a world class Judging system based on the AOS system to elevate African orchids. Together, we introduced the AOS to judging in Africa over the past 3 decades.

    A regular visitor to Japan and Taiwan, he has been a participating judge in most, if not all, AOS judgings during the past 10 years with Paul Bechtel and often with George Hatfield in Taiwan, and most recently at the NCKU University in November last year.

    His life's work has been in orchids and currently owns and run his own nursery, The Exotic Plant Company, in the Netherlands close to Rotterdam. He has registered close to 1,000 orchid hybrids with the RHS.

    He has authored and co-written some 15 books on various aspects of orchids, including Royal Botanic Gardens KEW monographs and checklist on Paphiopedilum, Cymbidium and Phragmipedium. He also co-authored The John Day Scrapbooks with Dr. Phillip Cribb.

    His book written in 2007, "ORCHIDS - A Practical Guide to Care and Cultivation," was adopted by the American Horticultural Society as the 'go to' book on orchids, selling 104,000 copies. Subsequent updates have been used extensively by learner judges with the latest intergeneric tables.

    He has a long association with the AOS, being an exhibitor for more than 10 years at the Greater New York Orchid Society until the shows were halted by 9/11. He has participated at the Philadelphia Flower Shows, the South Florida Orchid Shows, and several Spring and Fall Members' meetings where he exhibited and lectured on various topics including his conservation project in Rwanda. Several of his articles have been published in AOS Orchids magazine and many of his AOS award photographs have graced the covers over the years.

    He worked full-time with the WOC in Japan and Glasgow and on committees for the WOC in Johannesburg and now in Dresden. In 2014, his gold medal exhibit at the WOC featured the history of WOCs as he is probably the only person who has attended every WOC since the 8th WOC in Frankfurt in 1975. His exhibit in Dresden updated the history of WOCs that included memorabilia from the very first WOC. This was possible with the help of many members of the AOS, particularly the late Tom and Marion Sheehan and wider audience from around the world.

  • Robert Fuchs

    Bob Fuchs is a third-generation orchid grower, born and raised in Homestead, Florida. He has been involved in growing orchids since childhood.

    Bob was 10 years old at the time of his first experience collecting orchids in the wild. He accompanied his grandfather, Fred Fuchs, Sr., in exploring some of the many swamp and wilderness areas that were then abundant in south Florida. Grandpa Fuchs would tie a rope around Bob’s waist, attach it to his own belt, and off they’d go. Bob remembers falling many times, and his grandfather used the rope to pull him out. That rope served a double purpose; it prevented him from getting lost, and it kept him out of the ‘gator holes.

    He was 14 when he went on his first salvage collecting trip out of the country. Bob, his father Fred Fuchs, Jr., and two friends flew in a small, two-engine plane from Homestead airport to the Dominican Republic. The plane was running short on fuel, so they had to land in Santiago rather than go on to the capital Santo Domingo. They had been unable to contact the Santiago tower but landed anyway as the fuel problem was critical. The plane had an escort of military jeeps with soldiers holding machine guns, but when it stopped on the runway Bob’s father held out a bucket of Kentucky Fried Chicken to the soldiers. They smiled, put down their guns, escorted the Americans to the authorities, and then allowed them to stay and salvage orchids for three days.

    Since then, Bob has made many trips to Central and South America in search of orchids in the wild. On one of many trips to Nicaragua, he spotted an unusual orchid flowering on a large rock outcrop. He climbed through the ravines to get a better look and found a 4-foot diameter clump of the plant. He was sure the plant was a natural hybrid of Schomburgkia wendlandii and Laelia rubescens, so he photographed it carefully, collected a sample, and pressed an inflorescence for later identification. The pressed inflorescence was submitted to Marie Selby Botanical Gardens and was later identified as Schombolaelia fuchsii in honour of its discoverer. Two years later, when Bob returned to the same area to collect additional divisions of the plant, he discovered that the area had burned, and the original plant was lost forever.

    Bob and his father discovered another new species in Bolivia, Catasetum fuchsii.

    Bob’s interest in orchids grew, and by the time he graduated from high school, he had amassed quite a collection. His parents gave him a greenhouse as his graduation gift. His involvement with orchid societies began during this period, too. He and his three brothers placed their first orchid exhibit at the 1959 Miami Show, sponsored by the South Florida Orchid Society, and “The Fuchs Boys” display won a trophy. Bob joined the society as a member in 1960.

    After graduating from Florida State University in 1970, Bob embarked on a career in teaching. He taught Art at Homestead Junior High School and was chairman of the school’s Enrichment Department. On weekends and after school, he worked in his grandparents’ greenhouses trying to clean and renovate them and began his own orchid business on their property.

    After 15 years of teaching, Bob retired to devote his efforts full-time to R.F. Orchids and incorporated the business.

    Bob spent many years collecting and studying orchids in different areas of Central and South America. Currently, he is involved in conserving and perpetuating the native species of Southeast Asia with a breeding programme at his growing facility in Thailand. An accredited American Orchid Society judge, he travels extensively throughout the world to attend orchid shows, and present lectures and programmes to orchid societies and other groups, and he has written many articles for orchid publications. He is the author of the section on “Vanda Culture” in the American Orchid Society’s Handbook on Orchid Culture.

    In 2012, Bob was inducted into the Florida Agriculture Hall of Fame. He is the only orchid grower to receive this prestigious honour.

    He is a Life Member and former trustee and President of the American Orchid Society for the 2020-2022 term, a Life Member and past president of the South Florida Orchid Society as well as a recipient of its Distinguished Service Award. Because of his accomplishments in orchid hybridising, The Royal Horticultural Society of Thailand awarded him a Life Membership in their society. He is also a Life Member and past president of the East Everglades Orchid Society, and a member of the Royal Horticultural Society.
