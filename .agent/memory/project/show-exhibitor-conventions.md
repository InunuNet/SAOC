# Orchid Show Exhibitor Conventions — Research Reference

**Purpose**: designated input for F1 of mission `show-exhibitor-info`
(`.agent/memory/project/missions/2026-08-11-show-exhibitor-info.md`). This is a survey of
how established orchid shows worldwide brief their exhibitors (the people entering plants,
not the visiting public), so SAOC's committee can confirm/adjust a default structure
rather than invent one from scratch.

Method: Alembic (`localhost:7077`) web search + fetch only, read-only research, no
implementation. Sources cited per finding below.

---

## 1. Entry process & deadlines

- **Near-universal**: exhibitors register plants under an Exhibitor Number, which is
  matched against a published "Show Schedule" (a document listing every judging class).
  Registration can be online and/or on-site, with an entry cutoff before the show opens.
  Seen: Santa Barbara Intl Orchid Show (sborchidshow.com/plant-registration — online
  entries close ~Tue before show, or on-site until day before); Orchid Society of Western
  Pennsylvania (oswp.org/orchid-show-registration — separate Registration Instructions,
  Show Schedule, Entry Form, Genus/Class cross-reference list, all published ahead of
  time).
- **Near-universal**: correct classification (matching a plant to the right class) is
  explicitly the *exhibitor's* responsibility, not the show committee's. Judges/committee
  may reclassify or disqualify a misclassified entry. Source: AOS "A Guide to a Successful
  Orchid Show" (secure.aos.org/media/Content-Images/PDFs/Guide-to-a-Sucessful-Orchid-Show.pdf
  — PDF, text summarized via search snippet only, not directly fetchable through Alembic).
- **Common, not universal**: a distinction between "ribbon judging" (local/show-level) and
  formal accredited-body judging (AOS awards) as separate registrations, sometimes with
  separate fees. Seen: sborchidshow.com (Judging Entry/Registration Form, separate from
  plant registration); AOS judging FAQ (aos.org/awards-judging/judging-faqs) — describes
  AOS judging as open to non-members at a higher fee, judged shows requiring a separate
  application/fee process the host society files with an AOS Judging Center.
- **One-off / not established as a norm**: specific lead time for entry deadlines varies
  from same-week on-site registration to multi-week advance cutoffs — no consistent
  interval across shows. Do not bake in a specific number of days; leave for SAOC
  committee to set.
- **One-off**: fee structuring (per-entry vs per-exhibitor vs separate judging/trophy fee)
  is AOS-specific convention (show fee / trophy fee / award fee as distinct line items,
  aos.org/awards-judging) — not seen replicated outside AOS-affiliated shows in this
  survey. Treat as one model to offer, not the default.

## 2. Staging (setup, hall access, removal)

- **Near-universal**: plants are delivered and physically staged/benched during a fixed
  window *before* the show opens to the public, and the hall is closed to the public
  during that window. Seen: Horticultural Society of Canberra 2026 schedule
  (hsoc.org.au/.../Schedule_for_the_2026_Spring_Exhibition_and_Rose_Show.pdf — "All
  exhibits must be staged by 10am. The hall will be open for staging until 10pm Friday and
  from 7:30am Saturday"); Queensland Orchid Society general conditions (qos.org.au —
  "Entries will be accepted from 2pm and shall be benched by 7pm and available for judging
  by [set time]").
- **Near-universal**: plants must remain benched for the full run of the show; early
  removal is barred or strongly discouraged (implicit in every staging schedule found —
  none described a mechanism for early withdrawal). No source gave an explicit rationale
  in the text captured, but the convention (no removal before published closing time) is
  consistent across every schedule seen.
- **Not established from this survey**: exact staging duration (single evening vs 2 days)
  varies by show size — Canberra allows Friday evening through Saturday morning; smaller
  shows (QOS) compress registration/staging into a single afternoon. Scale to SAOC's own
  show size; don't assume a specific number of days.

## 3. Schedule of classes

- **Near-universal**: classes are organized by genus/alliance group (e.g. Laeliinae,
  Oncidiinae, Paphiopedilum, Phalaenopsis, Vandeae), each with individual-plant and
  multi-plant ("group of N") variants, plus catch-all "any other genus" classes. Seen in
  full detail: Orchid Society of Great Britain (osgb.org.uk/about-us/orchid-judging) —
  full Section A schedule of ~40 classes by alliance, plus Section B display classes.
  OSWP's genus/class cross-reference PDF exists specifically to help exhibitors map a
  plant to the right class number.
- **Common**: judges may split an oversubscribed class into sub-classes at their
  discretion (OSGB: "At the discretion of the Judges any class with numerous entries can
  be split up"; Australian club precedent similar — oscov.asn.au show-bench article
  describes subdividing e.g. Australian Native Dendrobium hybrids by breeding line when
  entries are numerous).
- **Common**: a "first-time exhibitor" or novice class exists as an on-ramp (OSGB class 39:
  "1 orchid species or hybrid, for a first-time First Prize winner in the Spring Show").

## 4. Judging

- **Near-universal**: judging happens after staging closes and before the public opening;
  exhibitors are generally not present in the room during judging deliberation. An
  explicit opt-out convention exists — a "Do Not Judge" card/sign an exhibitor can place
  on their own plant. Seen: Sunset Rotary AOS show rules (sunsetrotary.org/orchid-judging-
  rules — "Exhibitors who do not want AOS judging must display a 'Do Not Judge' sign").
- **Near-universal**: two judging tracks commonly coexist at larger shows — local
  ribbon/rosette placings (1st/2nd/3rd) decided by the host show, and separate formal
  accredited-body awards (AOS flower/cultural awards) decided by trained judges against a
  100-point scale, open to non-members at differential pricing. Seen: AOS judging FAQ
  (aos.org/awards-judging/judging-faqs); orchidnerd.com (AOS awards need a 90+/100 score
  for top awards). UK equivalent: OSGB — rosettes for 1st–3rd in every show class, separate
  club trophies for members only, certificates in lieu of trophy for non-member winners.
- **Common**: plants inside a group/society *display* are not automatically entered into
  individual judging unless the exhibitor separately marks and logs them (OSGB: "individual
  plants in societies' displays will not be judged unless specifically requested... marked
  by the exhibitors with special markers... entered into the Judging Record book").
- **Not established from this survey**: whether/how an exhibitor is notified of results
  before the public opening (e.g. posted results sheet vs individual notification) — no
  source gave a clear universal answer; flag as open.

## 5. Plant condition & eligibility rules

- **Near-universal**: entries must be free of pests and disease, subject to inspection,
  with disqualification/removal as the consequence of failing inspection. Seen: South
  Florida Orchid Society judging rules (sforchid.com/judging/judging-rules — "must be free
  of pests and disease"); Sunset Rotary AOS rules ("subject to inspection and may be
  disqualified and will be removed"); OSGB ("entrant's responsibility to make sure... free
  from pests and diseases").
- **Near-universal**: correct labelling/nomenclature is required — full genus/hybrid name
  and parentage for hybrids. Where a hybrid isn't formally registered, "trade name or genus
  name + the word 'hybrid'" is an accepted fallback (OSGB). AOS judging FAQ likewise
  requires "complete species or hybrid name, and the cross (if a hybrid)" to be judged.
- **Common, one clear precedent not confirmed as universal**: a minimum
  ownership/possession duration before a plant is eligible in certain classes — OSGB
  requires the entrant to have had the plant "in the entrant's possession for at least 12
  months" for its main Section A classes, applying to both in-person and online entries.
  This specific "12 months" figure is a UK/OSGB convention; treat the *concept* (a
  grown-by/owned-by-exhibitor duration rule, common in AOS cultural-award language too) as
  near-universal in spirit, but the exact duration as show-specific — leave the number for
  SAOC's committee.

## 6. Display / stand rules

- **Common**: society/group displays are judged as a distinct category from individual
  plant classes, usually with a defined space allocation and a cap on non-orchid
  ("ornamental") material as a percentage of the exhibit. Seen: OSGB Section B ("A display
  of orchids and ornamental plants, space allowed up to 1m x 2m; ornamental plants must not
  exceed 50% of the exhibit"; same 50% cap repeated in Section A class 38 for smaller
  group entries).
- **Not established from this survey**: who is permitted to *build* a display stand
  (professional vs member-only), height limits, or structural/material restrictions — no
  source gave concrete rules on this; flag as a real gap, not a convention to assume.

## 7. Sales

- **Near-universal**: retail plant sales are kept structurally separate from the
  competitive judged floor — a distinct vendor/trade area with its own setup process, not
  folded into exhibitor judging entries. Seen: Taiwan International Orchid Show (tios.tw —
  described as combining an "orchid gardening market" alongside the judged exhibition, in
  a separate zone); Santa Barbara Intl Orchid Show references vendor registration
  distinctly from plant judging registration; a South African "Orchids & More" plant-show
  programme page (found via search snippet only, site now deleted — orchidsandmore2023
  .wordpress.com — could not be re-verified, treat this one as unconfirmed) described
  separate "vendor set-up" and "registrant" (competitive exhibitor) tracks.
- **Not established from this survey**: no source described a commission-based
  consignment-sales model at the judged competitive show itself — sales appear to be
  direct vendor-to-public in every case surveyed. Whether SAOC exhibitors may sell plants
  directly from their competitive entries was not addressed by any source; flag as open.

## 8. Practicalities (insurance, security, watering, parking)

- **Weak/no confirmed convention from public sources.** Parking/access logistics are
  addressed only at the visitor level (e.g. TIOS venue driving/transit directions,
  ezgo.ardswc.gov.tw), not as exhibitor-specific loading/access instructions. Overnight
  security of benched plants, day-to-day watering/care responsibility during the show run,
  and insurance/liability terms were **not found in any exhibitor-facing schedule or rules
  page surveyed** — these are presumably handled in internal show-committee
  documentation, not published exhibitor copy. **This is a genuine gap in the public
  record, not a "no convention exists" finding** — do not assume any of these are
  standardized; they need direct input from SAOC's own show committee/past show chairs.

## 9. Cross-cutting near-universal pattern worth calling out explicitly

Every established show separates exhibitor-facing information into distinct documents/
sections rather than one blob: (a) registration/entry instructions, (b) the show
schedule/class list, (c) rules/eligibility conditions, (d) trophy/award list. See OSWP's
document set (oswp.org/orchid-show-registration) as the clearest single example of this
separation. **This maps directly onto the current schema gap** — see Section 10.

## 10. What already exists in the codebase vs the gap

- `app/(marketing)/national-show/exhibitors/page.tsx` (~108 lines): a hero section plus 4
  hardcoded `INFO_BLOCKS` — "Who can exhibit," "Entry categories," "Setup and staging,"
  "Judging" — each a single placeholder paragraph, followed by a "Coming soon 2026" CTA
  section directing users to Contact Us. There is no entry-deadline data, no fee data, no
  plant-condition/labelling rules, no display/stand rules, no sales policy, and no
  practicalities section (parking/security/watering/insurance) anywhere on the page.
- `sanity/schemas/documents/nationalShow.ts`: the only exhibitor-relevant field is
  `exhibitorStages`, typed as a generic `portableText` blob — unstructured free text with
  no sub-fields for deadlines, fees, staging times, class-schedule reference, condition
  rules, or display rules. Every other field on this schema is show logistics
  (title/date/location/hero/countdown) or the ticketing `salesOpen` gate — unrelated to
  exhibitor rules.
- **The gap is total**: there is no structured schema matching any of the 8 sections
  above. An editor today can only paste one undifferentiated block of text into
  `exhibitorStages`. Given the cross-cutting pattern in Section 9 — every real show
  separates these into distinct documents/sections — the natural fix is to give each of
  Sections 1–8 above its own field/object in the schema, not to keep expanding one
  portableText blob.

## 11. South African divergence — questions for the committee, not rules to assume

These are flagged as **open questions**, not established facts to encode as copy:

- **CITES**: verified (CapeNature — capenature.co.za/permits/cites-permits; SAFLII CITES
  Regulations 2010 — saflii.org/za/legis/consol_reg/coitiesowfafr2010832) that CITES
  permits apply to *international* import/export/re-export of Appendix-II-listed orchid
  specimens crossing SA's borders — not to domestic transport of cultivated plants between
  provinces for a national show. Artificially propagated hybrids of Cattleya, Cymbidium,
  Dendrobium (phalaenopsis & nobile types), Oncidium, Phalaenopsis, Vanda and their
  intergenerics are explicitly exempted from CITES permit requirements even
  internationally, under conditions (cites.org CITES World newsletter #9 PDF, CITES
  Primer on importing/exporting). **Question for committee**: confirm no exhibitor-facing
  CITES paperwork is implied for domestic entries — only relevant if a foreign exhibitor
  imports plants into SA for the show.
- **Provincial/domestic plant-movement permits**: no interprovincial biosecurity or
  plant-movement permit regime specific to orchids was found in this search pass.
  **Question for committee**: does any domestic movement-of-plants permit apply when
  members transport orchids across provincial lines to a national show (e.g. under
  agricultural/biosecurity regulation unrelated to CITES)? Not established either way —
  don't assume none exists just because this search didn't surface one.
- **Indigenous-species restrictions**: not investigated in depth in this pass beyond
  confirming the CITES international-only scope above. SAOC's own scope boundary is
  orchids *in cultivation* (per project CLAUDE.md) — wild-orchid conservation and
  indigenous-species protection belong to WOSA, not SAOC. **Question for committee**: if
  indigenous SA species (e.g. propagated Disa, Eulophia) are exhibited, confirm whether any
  domestic permit/provenance-documentation requirement applies distinct from CITES (e.g.
  provincial nature conservation ordinances governing propagated indigenous flora) — this
  was not resolved by the sources found and needs direct confirmation, not assumption.
- **South African show precedent**: could not locate any archived SAOC national-show
  exhibitor pack, entry form, or rules PDF online. Only confirmed: the National Show
  rotates provinces every 3 years (witsorchid.co.za); the 18th National Show was held in
  KZN in September 2024 (Facebook event page, krugerpark.co.za news mention) with no rules
  content indexed. Provincial society sites (Cape Orchid Society — capeorchidsociety.co.za;
  Witwatersrand Orchid Society — witsorchid.co.za) show only local monthly-meeting/
  autumn-spring show culture (member plant tables, sales tables), not judged national-show
  rule precedent. **Recommendation**: request SAOC's own past national-show exhibitor
  packs directly from the council/committee — they are the authoritative local precedent
  and were not discoverable via web search.

## 12. Explicit list of things this research could NOT establish

- Exact standard entry-deadline lead time (varies too widely to generalize).
- A universal fee-structuring model outside AOS-affiliated shows.
- How exhibitors are notified of judging results before public opening.
- Who may build a display stand, height limits, or structural/material rules for displays.
- Whether exhibitors may sell directly from their competitive entries (no source
  addressed this either way).
- Insurance/liability terms, overnight security arrangements, and day-to-day
  watering/care responsibility during a show run — not found in any public exhibitor-
  facing document surveyed.
- Any SAOC-specific historical exhibitor pack or entry form (not locatable via web
  search — needs direct request to the council).
- Domestic (non-CITES) plant-movement permit requirements within South Africa.
- Indigenous-species-specific documentation requirements for propagated SA species like
  Disa or Eulophia at a national show.
