### /refunds — PASS
Both breakpoints look finished and well-designed, not a dumped legal document. Hero image + "LEGAL" pill + serif H1 reads as a designed page. Body has real typographic hierarchy: bolded section headers (Terms pending confirmation, Refunds, Cancellations, Admission tickets, Conference tickets, Workshop/field trip/cocktail tickets, Vendor stand bookings, Exceptional circumstances, How to request a refund), short paragraphs, and a bulleted tiered-notice schedule rather than one wall of prose. The grey "Draft pending legal review" notice box is clearly set off from body copy.
Provisional-figure disclosure check (specifically requested): confirmed legible on both viewports. Every dollar/day figure that's an estimate (100% refund, thirty days, 90% refund, fourteen and thirty days, 50% refund, fourteen days, 0% refund, seven days) carries a small caps "PROVISIONAL" superscript/badge directly next to it, rendered in a lighter weight with a subtle background chip on desktop — easy for a human eye to spot, not just present in markup. No ambiguity about which figures are firm vs. estimated.
Mobile (390): no overflow, no overlap, footer columns stack cleanly, nav collapses to hamburger correctly, "PROVISIONAL" tags wrap onto their own line under the stat but remain fully legible.
No broken/overlapping/unstyled elements found. Looks like a page a real orchid society would be proud of.

Flagging as a content-completeness observation, not a visual FAIL, since this route was not one of the four assigned policy pages and I was not asked to assess data completeness — just noting it since it was visibly thin compared to the four policy pages, which are fully fleshed out.

### Overall
All 4 assigned policy routes (/refunds, /constitution, /privacy, /terms) PASS the visual bar on both mobile (390) and desktop (1280). No horizontal overflow, no overlapping elements, no unstyled/broken markup, no walls of undifferentiated legal text — every long-form page has real section headers and short paragraphs/bullets. The refunds provisional-figure disclosure specifically reads clearly to a human eye on both viewports. Nothing to fix on these four routes from a visual-QA perspective.
